**Food safety:** cold food at 5 °C or below; hot holding at about 63 °C or above; poultry and minced meat cooked to a core temperature of about 74 °C. These are given "or as the Centre's food safety plan says". Food handlers need a Food Handler's Permit from the local health department.

## Kitchen units (THHCFP0321D poultry, THHCFP0331D meat and seafood, THHCFP0651D vegetables/fruit/eggs/farinaceous, THHCFP0271D appetizers and salads, THHCFP0251D cleaning, THHCFP1031B knives, THHCFP0671C stocks/sauces/soups)
- **Washing raw chicken:** poultry question 6 and essay 1 say not to rinse raw chicken under a running tap, because splashes spread bacteria. This is accepted food-safety advice, but it goes against the common habit of washing chicken with lime. Check that the wording suits how the Centre teaches it.
- **Board colours:** only red (raw meat), blue (raw fish) and green (salad and fruit) are asked, each "or as the Centre's code says". Raw poultry is "the Centre's colour", because the yellow code differs between systems.
- **Frying oil:** about 160–180 °C; about 170–180 °C for saltfish fritters.
- **Cooling:** no exact time is given ("within the time the Centre's food safety plan gives"). Reheat once only, to about 74 °C core. Freezers at -18 °C or below.
- **Knife cuts:** brunoise and julienne about 2–3 mm, macédoine about 5 mm, paysanne about 1 cm. Honing angle about 15–20°. Check against the Centre's standard; Practical 1's sizes come on the assessor's card.
- **Stocks and sauces:**
  - fish stock about 20 minutes; white chicken stock 2–3 hours; brown stock 6–8 hours;
  - béchamel 100 g butter and 100 g flour per litre of milk, which gives a thick sauce. Confirm the ratio the Centre wants;
  - pasta: about 1 litre of water per 100 g.
- **Kidney beans:** soak, throw away the water, boil hard in fresh water, then simmer.
- **Hollandaise:** keep warm in small batches for a short time only. Confirm the Centre's holding time.
- **Fish:** a reef-fish toxin (barracuda) that cooking cannot destroy, and histamine in warm tuna or mackerel.
- **Practicals need things done the day before:** red peas soaked overnight, saltfish soaked, and optionally pre-seasoned meat.
- Only a trainee the assessor has trained cleans the slicer.

## THHPAT0542C cakes and puddings; THHPAT0532C pastries; THHPAT0552C yeast goods
- Oven ranges, all "as the recipe and oven maker say":
  - sponge about 170–190 °C;
  - sweet potato pudding about 160–180 °C;
  - puff pastry about 200–220 °C;
  - bread rolls about 200–230 °C;
  - doughnut oil about 170–190 °C.
- Cakes and puddings:
  - molasses is the acid that makes bicarbonate of soda work;
  - bulla and gingerbread are melting-method cakes;
  - royal icing goes on Jamaican wedding and Christmas cakes.
- Pastries:
  - small-tart pastry is rolled to about 3 mm;
  - full puff uses about the same weight of fat as flour;
  - patty pastry is coloured with turmeric or curry powder.
- Bun (question 28, short answer 15): some recipes use yeast and others baking powder. Check these against the Centre's recipe.
- Bread goes stale faster in the fridge. A cut finger gets a blue plaster.
- The practicals assume a training kitchen with mixers, a prover and a fryer, plus a sewing machine and training money with a mock card terminal.
