# B032: facts for an instructor to check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 3 kits in
`study-kits/electrical-installation-level-3/`, all `"status": "to-check"`.
Figures that vary by maker, code or job are given in the kits as "typical"
or "about", with "follow the specification / the maker / your instructor".
All sums were worked twice. The kits give both NEC (US) and IEC/British
terms; please confirm which test tables and limits the Centre teaches.

## MEMCOR0051A Perform related computations – basic

- Correction factors quoted as "one common type of PVC cable": 0.87 at
  40 °C, 0.71 at 50 °C; grouping 0.80 (2 circuits) and 0.70 (3 bunched);
  thermal insulation "can be 0.5 or less". These are IEC/British table
  values; swap in the Centre's own table if it differs.
- NEC continuous-load rule (breaker at 125% of a load running 3 h or more,
  such as a storage water heater) is stated as a note. Case c1 therefore says
  the designer applies Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz "with no other allowance", so that the
  25 A answer to P-02 stands. Check you are happy with that framing.
- Breaker sizes "15, 20, 30, 40 A (NEC-style panels); 16, 20, 25, 32, 40 A
  (IEC-style)". P-02 keys 25 A; NEC panels do offer 25 A, but it is less
  common in Jamaican panels.
- Voltage-drop limit "often about 3% for a final circuit; some designs up to
  5% from the intake". Conductor resistance 0.0046 Ω/m is given as data.
- Copper ρ = 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m, aluminium about 2.8 × 10⁻⁸; copper about 0.4%
  per °C; AWG areas (14 = 2.1, 12 = 3.3, 10 = 5.3, 8 = 8.4 mm²).
- NEC conduit fill 40% for three or more conductors; trunking limit "varies"
  (P-19 uses 45% as a specification figure).
- Motor starting current "often about 5 to 8 times" running; slip "2% to 6%";
  60 Hz motors run about one-sixth slower on 50 Hz and "can overheat".
- Lead-acid usable depth of discharge "about half" (maker's figure in c4).
- Jamaica: JPS bill described as customer charge + energy charge + fuel
  charge; "larger commercial customers are also billed for their maximum
  demand". Electricity rates (J$60–J$65/kWh) and all prices are examples.
  GCT is "at the current rate" only, no figure.
- Exam bank: no errors found. It uses IEC-style breaker sizes (16 A, 20 A)
  and "RCD"; the kit covers both systems.

## MEMCOR0063A Attend to breakdown in hazardous area

- Explosive ranges (approximate): methane 4.4–17%, LPG "about 2% to 10%",
  petrol vapour about 1.4–7.6%, hydrogen 4–77%, ethanol about 3–19%.
  Flash points: petrol about −40 °C; diesel "usually above about 52 °C".
- Gas groups and T classes: methane IIA T1, propane IIA T1, butane IIA T2,
  petrol IIA T3, hydrogen IIC T1. Ethanol/spirit group is deliberately NOT
  given ("take it from the site's drawing") because sources differ.
- NEC groups: A, B ≈ IIC; C ≈ IIB; D (petrol, LPG) ≈ IIA. Division 1 ≈
  Zones 0 + 1; Division 2 ≈ Zone 2.
- EPL/zone and ATEX category tables; default ambient −20 °C to +40 °C;
  Ex e enclosures "at least IP54"; Ex d threaded joints "commonly at least
  five full threads"; no stopping plug in a thread adaptor; flanged
  flamepaths kept clear of obstructions "as the installation rules require"
  (no distances given).
- Gas detectors: pellistors need oxygen and can be poisoned; IR sensors do
  not detect hydrogen; oxygen alarms "commonly about 19.5% and 23.5%";
  work limit "many sites use 10% LEL or less", lower for hot work.
- IS: light-blue cable/terminals; zener barrier needs a "sound,
  low-resistance IS earth" (no ohm figure given); some live IS work allowed
  only under site rules.
- Isolation of all live conductors including the neutral (IEC 60079-14
  practice); proving dead only with instruments the permit allows.
- Jamaica: list of hazardous workplaces (fuel, LPG, distilleries, sugar
  factories during the crop, mills, sewage, spray booths, natural gas).
  Heat above 40 °C and salt air are flagged as local issues.
- Exam bank: T2-E03 and T1-26 treat a light "under a fuel station canopy"
  as Zone 2. Under common forecourt practice canopy lights are often
  outside the classified area; the bank's "as the drawing shows" wording
  makes it acceptable, but students may ask.

## MEMCOR0071A Use electrical/electronic measuring devices

- Insulation test table (IEC/British): SELV/PELV 250 V DC, min 0.5 MΩ;
  up to 500 V 500 V DC, min 1 MΩ; above 500 V 1 000 V DC, min 1 MΩ. P-02
  keys 250 V "under the IEC test tables". Confirm the Centre's figures.
- NEC single-rod rule: a rod over 25 Ω must be supplemented by a second
  electrode at least 1.8 m (6 ft) away (P-09). Fall-of-potential "62%" rule
  and ±10% check positions.
- RCD trip time "usual limit 300 ms at rated current for a 30 mA RCD";
  GFCIs "follow their own standard". Continuity tester test current "about
  200 mA". GS38 probe tips "no more than about 4 mm".
- CAT III 300 V stated as the minimum for 110/220 V board work.
- Plug-in GFCI testers cannot trip a GFCI on an outlet with no earth (P-06).
- Cable table value 19.5 mΩ/m (line + protective conductor) is given as
  data in case c1.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0081A Mark off/out (general engineering)

- Typical heights (specification decides): switches about 1 200–1 400 mm,
  outlets 300–450 mm, worktop outlets 150–200 mm above the worktop.
- Device sizes: 96 × 96 mm meter → about 92 × 92 mm cut-out; "22 mm" push
  buttons need about 22.3–22.5 mm; a "½ inch" conduit knockout is about
  22 mm across. All say "check the maker's sheet".
- Conduit offset multipliers (1 ÷ sin angle): 10° 5.76, 22.5° 2.61,
  30° 2.00, 45° 1.41, 60° 1.15.
- Line-laser accuracy check is described generally ("as its maker
  describes ... turning through 180°"); please show the Centre's method.
- "Safe zones" are explained as British practice (in line with accessories,
  within about 150 mm of a wall top or corner), with a note that Jamaican
  conduit in block and slabs may not follow any zone.
- Exam bank: no errors found. T2-S05 ("safe zone") is a British/IEC term
  not used in the NEC; the kit explains this.
