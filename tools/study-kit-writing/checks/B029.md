# B029: facts for an instructor to check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 2 kits in
`study-kits/electrical-installation/`, all `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that
vary by maker or job are written as "typical" or "about", with "follow the
drawing, the maker and your instructor". People, businesses and cases are
fictional. NEC figures are quoted as the kits give them; check them against the
code edition and local amendments the Centre uses.

## MEMINS0071D Prepare for electrical conduits/wiring installation

- NEC figures: no more than four quarter bends (360°) between pull points;
  40% conduit fill for three or more conductors; small PVC conduit (1/2–1 in)
  supported within about 0.9 m of each box and every 0.9 m; EMT within 0.9 m
  of boxes and every 3 m.
- PVC expansion "about 0.05 mm per metre per °C" (10 m × 30 °C ≈ 15 mm;
  case c3: 18 m ≈ 27 mm).
- Cable guide "14 AWG 15 A, 12 AWG 20 A, 10 AWG 30 A"; colours black/red,
  white/grey, green/green-yellow/bare.
- Typical heights: outlets about 300–450 mm, switches about 1.2 m above FFL;
  builder's datum "often 1 m above FFL".
- Symbols: S3 = three-way switch (British two-way); duplex outlet as a circle
  with two lines. Check against the drawings the Centre uses.
- Take-off allowances: 150–300 mm per conductor at each box; 5–10% waste.
- Dust mask "P2 or N95 class or better" for chasing; 3 m from overhead lines
  (as in the pilot kit).
- Exam bank: T1-16 and the T2-E05 model answer treat wooden ladders as fine
  for electrical work; the approved pilot (EEMELS0011B) says wood is not
  recommended. The kit teaches fibreglass only.

## MEMINS0092D Install electrical and electronic apparatus, machinery, fixtures and secondary wiring

- NEC: fan boxes listed and marked for fan support; luminaires over 23 kg
  (50 lb) supported independently of the box unless the box is rated; motor
  disconnect normally within sight of the motor.
- Fan blades "at least 2.1 m" above the floor (maker guidance).
- V-belt tension rule "about 16 mm deflection per metre of span"; re-tension
  after the first hours of running.
- A 60 Hz-only motor on 50 Hz "about one-sixth slower, may overheat".
- Insulated crimp colours: red 0.5–1.5, blue 1.5–2.5, yellow 4–6 mm²;
  three-wire DC sensors brown +, blue −, black output.
- Anti-static strap resistor "about 1 MΩ".
- Insulation test "often 500 V DC"; "below 1 MΩ is a fail" given as a guide
  from British practice (the NEC sets no general figure). Check the Centre's
  test sheet.
- IP digit meanings and NEMA 1 / 3R / 4X examples.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMINS0162C Cut, fit and install trunking system

- Space factor: 45% (British practice) and 20% for NEC wireways are both
  given; the kit tells trainees to follow the rule the drawing sets. Worked
  examples: 50 × 50 mm inside area "about 2,200 mm²"; case c1 compartment
  1,400 mm² at 45% with 25 mm² cables = 25 cables.
- Hacksaw: "thinner metal, finer blade", about 32 TPI for sheet trunking.
- NEC names: "wireway" and "surface raceway".
- Case c2's 25 mm stop-short for the internal angle and the 600 mm fixing
  spacing in the notes are illustrative, not from a real maker.
- Whether steel trunking may serve as the protective conductor is left to
  "the code and the drawing".
- Burning PVC gives off hydrogen chloride.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMINS0172C Prepare and install basic cable trays

- Splices "within about a quarter of the span" of a support, and supports
  near each end of fittings: common maker guidance; check the Centre's system.
- "On ladder tray, cut where the maker allows, so a rung is close to the cut
  end": check against the maker.
- Typical sizes: widths 100–900 mm, side rails 50–150 mm, 3 m lengths;
  strut channel about 41 × 41 mm; stainless "often grade 316".
- Bending radius example 8 × D (illustrative; makers' multiples vary).
- Wire mesh tray cut with bolt cutters close to a weld.
- Tray as an earth conductor: "depends on the code, the tray's marking and
  the drawing".
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMINS0262D Install distribution panels, metering sockets, terminal mains and meter earthing systems

- Who wires from the weatherhead to the meter socket's line terminals: the
  kit says it varies and to follow JPS's current requirements; the training
  board keeps them empty and labelled (as the bank does). Check what JPS
  currently expects.
- No meter-socket height is given ("the height JPS's requirements give"):
  the instructor may add JPS's current figure.
- Inspection by "a licensed electrical inspector, under the Government
  Electrical Regulator", then JPS connects (as in the pilot kit).
- NEC figures: working space about 900 × 750 mm × 2 m high, door opening
  90°; highest breaker handle about 2 m; no panels in bathrooms, clothes
  closets or over steps; torque tool required where a value is given;
  re-marking large conductors white/grey (neutral) and green (earth);
  storage water heater sized at 125% (3,300 W → 15 A → 20 A double-pole,
  12 AWG).
- Earthing: rod about 2.4 m × 16 mm copper-clad; NEC 25 Ω single-rod rule;
  second rod at least about 1.8 m away; rock: angled rod or trench; steel
  conduit round the earthing conductor bonded at both ends; 62% spike rule;
  dry season "roughly December to April".
- Neutral-earth bond: "in the main panel (and, where the drawing shows it, at
  the meter socket)". Check local practice and the inspector's view.
- Neutral current = difference of the line currents (stated for simple
  resistive loads only).
- Exam bank: the T2-E04 model answer and practical 1 run a separate earth
  conductor in the terminal mains from the meter socket to the earth bar.
  Under the NEC the service neutral normally carries fault current between
  the meter and the main disconnect, so a separate earth there is local or
  designer practice. The kit says "where the drawing includes one".
