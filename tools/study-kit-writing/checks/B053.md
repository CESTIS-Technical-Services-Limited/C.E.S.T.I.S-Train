# B053 checks for the instructor

## CRICUS0012A Provide quality customer/client service
- Skin (patch) test timing "often 24 to 48 hours before" is a general guide; the exam bank essay says about 48 hours. Confirm the Centre's rule and the product makers' instructions.
- Jamaica's Data Protection Act is mentioned only by purpose (collect only what is needed, keep secure, do not share). No sections or dates are given. Confirm the wording the Centre uses.
- Discrimination is said to be wrong and "may be against the law", with no Act named. Check whether the Centre wants a named Jamaican law (for example on disability or employment) added.
- The "service standards" figures (greet within one minute, tell clients waiting more than ten minutes) are examples only; each salon sets its own.
- Seventh-day Adventist Saturday worship, Rastafari locs and Muslim modesty needs are given as examples of cultural and religious requirements; confirm they suit the intake.
- Average-score worked example (6 clients: 28, 17, 29, 22 totals) and the averages 4.7, 2.8, 4.8, 3.7 rounded to one decimal place.
- Exam bank: T1-35 ("remove the mask at once with cool water") is fine for a burning mask, but the product maker's instruction should decide the rinse temperature. T2-S12 average (4) is correct. Several bank options are very weak distractors (for example T1-23, T1-24).

## LMFIND0112B Research interior decoration and design influences
- Colour temperature figures (warm about 2700-3000 K, neutral about 4000 K, daylight about 5000-6500 K) and "aim for a CRI of 90 or more" for colour work are typical industry guides, not Jamaican standards.
- Paint cover (about 10 m2 per litre), tile waste (about 10%) and the example tile price (J$2,800 per m2) are practice figures only; products and suppliers vary.
- Georgian dates (about 1714 to 1830) and the Victorian, Art Nouveau, Art Deco, mid-century dates are the usual British and international dates; Jamaican buildings followed with a delay.
- Jamaican claims to confirm: Georgian buildings in Falmouth and Spanish Town; hip roofs and louvres as responses to hurricanes and heat; Taino, Indian, Chinese and Middle Eastern influences described only in general terms (round shapes, woven crafts, textiles); Jamaica National Heritage Trust and Institute of Jamaica as research sources.
- Exam bank: T1-22 ("light, cool colours help a small room look bigger") and T1-43 (mirrors make a room look bigger) are general tendencies, not rules; T1-21 includes a near-complementary option (purple with lemon yellow) which could confuse trainees; T2-E05 describes warm colours as "seeming to come towards you" which is a tendency only.
- No building code, ramp slope or fire figures are given: the notes tell trainees to ask the instructor for the Centre's safety and building guidance.

## THHFRO0032B Develop and apply conversational skills in a foreign language
- All Spanish should be checked by a Spanish speaker. The kit uses the Spanish most common across Latin America and Spain: seseo (c and z as s), 'usted' forms, 'manicura' and 'manicure', and 'tinte' for colour. Words that vary by country: flequillo/cerquillo/fleco (fringe), secador, 'el lavabo' for the basin, 'mascarilla'.
- "Jamaican" is given as 'jamaicano' (with 'jamaiquino' also understood); confirm the form the Centre prefers.
- 'No la oigo bien' on the telephone assumes a female caller; 'No le oigo bien' is also used.
- Time and numbers: 'cinco menos cuarto' = 4:45; 'seis mil doscientos' = 6,200; 'doce mil quinientos' = 12,500; 'nueve mil trescientos' = 9,300.
- Skin test timing before a colour (often 24 to 48 hours) follows the maker's instructions; the bank essay says about 48 hours.
- Exam bank: T1-28 'en quince días' is the idiom for 'in two weeks', not literally fifteen days, and the question lists it as "in fifteen days"; T2-S11 accepts '20 veinte', and T2-S08 accepts 'shampoo' (an English spelling); T2-S03 accepts 'una' and 'la una' for 'uñas', which are different words (one and one o'clock). T2-E02 says 'Buenas tardes' in the afternoon, which is right, but the bank has no question on greeting by time of day.
- Hotels with Spanish-speaking guests: the kit says only that some resort hotels have such guests; confirm the claim suits the Centre's area.
