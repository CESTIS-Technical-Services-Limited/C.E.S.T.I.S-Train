# Batch B042: what an instructor should check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 3 kits, all
`"status": "to-check"`. Sums use data the bank and kit give; where the real
figure comes from a code table or the Centre's data, the kit says so.

## MEMPLN0063A Coordinate and manage basic installation projects

- Jamaican public holidays used as examples: Labour Day 23 May, Emancipation
  Day 1 August, Independence Day 6 August; rainy seasons "roughly May-June
  and September-November".
- Government inspection by a licensed inspector under the Government
  Electrical Regulator (GER) before JPS connects; JPS needs an application
  and "may need weeks" for a new service (no fixed time is given).
- Block-house sequence (conduit built in or chased before plaster; second fix
  after first coat of paint) follows the bank's T1-04 and T2-E02; check it
  against local practice.
- Permit to work described generally (never allows live work).
- Exam bank: T1-14 ("60% complete, 80% of hours used") is only a rough
  indicator; no errors found in the sums.

## MEMPLN0094A Determine and plan for electrical installation requirements

- Demand factors (0.65 for the house example, 0.6 in the bakery case) are
  invented teaching figures; the kit tells trainees to use the factors the
  specification or instructor gives.
- Line current "demand W ÷ 220" assumes a balanced split-phase load; the
  100 A service in the house example is a teaching choice, not a JPS rule.
- Fixed PV faces south in Jamaica (as the bank has it), at a low tilt.
- Waste allowance "often about 10%" (bank uses 10%); 8 W/m2 lighting
  allowance, tariff J$55/60 per kWh are bank-style teaching figures.
- Special-location list (pool, bathroom, kitchen) refers to "the code the
  Centre uses"; no distances or clauses are given.
- Exam bank: T1-12 and T1-30 mix IEC (RCD) and NEC (GFCI) ideas; T2-E04's
  "derating" and T1-33 are fine. T1-37 (1 980 W at 110 V = 18 A, so 2
  circuits of 10 A) is correct; the "least number" wording is the only catch.

## MEMPLN0104A Interpret and carry out electrical design

- The kit teaches the simple rule Ib <= In <= Iz with ratings multiplied by
  correction factors (as the bank does). The NEC treats continuous loads,
  motors and breaker rounding differently: confirm what the Centre uses.
- The bank uses IEC terms (TN-C-S, TT, Zs, RCBO, 30 mA RCD) while Jamaica
  follows the NEC (neutral grounded at the service; GFCI about 5 mA). The kit
  gives both. Check the earthing-system table with the Centre's code.
- Volt-drop limits (3% branch, 5% overall) and the 300 mA time-delayed RCD
  upstream of 30 mA devices are typical, not code quotations.
- Capacitor sums: 60 kW 0.8 -> 0.95 needs about 25 kVAR; bank answers checked
  (T1-14 21.1 kVAR, T2-E05 about 44 kVAR) and are correct.
- Hold-in contact description (NC stop, NO start, NO auxiliary in parallel).
- MCA/MOP explained as US nameplate terms.
- Exam bank: no errors found (T1-05, T1-20, T1-21, T1-32, T1-43 re-worked).

## MEMPLN0113A Plan for wiring and installation of appliances and fixtures

- Currents use I = P / V for resistive loads; for motors the nameplate
  current is used. Water and shower heaters may need a breaker above
  "just above the load" under the NEC (continuous-load rule): the kit says
  "the maker's rating".
- Dead tests: visual, protective-conductor continuity, conductor continuity,
  insulation resistance, polarity; sensitive electronics disconnected for the
  insulation test (typical test voltages 250/500 V; no pass figure is given).
- Bank T1-13 and T1-44 say indoor-only switches are unsuitable outdoors; the
  kit agrees. T1-45 "supervisor and, where needed, the client" is as the bank
  has it.
- Exam bank: T1-25 (A/C materials list) and T2-E05 (fan on a single-pole
  breaker) are fine; no errors found.

## MEMPLN0114A Evaluate electrical installation

- Thermal "bands" are described only in general terms (small, moderate,
  large delta T); no °C limits are given. Check against the Centre's survey
  rules.
- Statement that heat in a joint rises roughly with the square of current.
- The kit says flood-damaged boards, breakers, outlets and RCDs "should
  normally be replaced or checked as the maker says".
- PV modules make voltage in daylight even when broken.
- Load factor = average demand / maximum demand (bank's definition); the
  bank's "maximum demand" in T2-S06 is the highest average over a short
  period (about 15 minutes) on some bills.
- Exam bank: T1-07 (52 A and 30 A gives about 22 A neutral) is correct for
  resistive loads only; T2-E04 sums (14 352 kWh, J$861 120, 2.1 years) check
  out. T1-35 (earth 12 -> 85 ohm) is fine.
