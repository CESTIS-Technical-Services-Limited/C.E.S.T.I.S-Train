# Batch B041: what an instructor should check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 3 kits, all `"status": "to-check"`.
Figures that vary by maker or job are written as typical. Case-study figures
(kVA, mV/A/m, timings, resistances) are invented for the exercises and were
worked twice. The points below are the ones the writer was least sure of.

## MEMMRD0693A Coordinate the installation of electrical equipment, ancillary apparatus and secondary wiring

- IP code table and NEMA types (1, 3R, 4, 4X, 12) are from the standard
  descriptions; confirm which one the Centre teaches.
- Working space in front of boards: "about 0.9 m deep, 762 mm wide, 2 m high"
  and highest operating handle "about 2 m" (NEC 110.26 style); the kit says to
  take exact figures from the code edition used.
- Wire colours: US (NFPA 79 style) and IEC (IEC 60204-1 style) columns,
  including orange for circuits that stay live with the panel isolator off
  ("yellow on older panels" for the US). Check against the Centre's practice.
- "Duct fill about two-thirds" and "wiring duct, 35 mm DIN rail" are common
  panel-shop rules of thumb, not code figures.
- Discharge times: about a minute for a correction bank and 5 to 15 minutes
  for a drive; the kit tells the trainee to use the label.
- A CT secondary is "never fused" and is shorted, not opened, before the
  meter is removed; the 400/5 and 800/5 examples were worked twice.
- Spare capacity "often 10 to 20%"; 24 V control transformers "63 VA or 100 VA"
  are examples only.
- Exam bank: T1-45 (4 coils × 12 VA + 6 lamps × 2 VA = 60 VA) is correct as
  holding VA only; the kit adds that inrush needs the maker's tables.

## MEMMRD0703A Coordinate the installation of substation plant and apparatus

- Jamaican claims: JPS distribution at "for example 12 kV or 24 kV", sub-transmission
  and transmission at 69 kV and 138 kV; the case studies use 24 kV. Confirm the
  voltages the Centre quotes. Government Electrical Regulator (GER)
  certification before JPS connects.
- Earthing: the kit says a substation's design resistance is set by the
  designer and is usually far lower than the 25 ohms the NEC accepts for a
  single rod. The case figure of "not more than 2 ohms" is invented.
- Sling angle table (0.50, 0.58, 0.71, 1.00 per leg at 0°, 60°, 90°, 120°) and
  "many plans allow no more than 90° between legs".
- MV cable minimum bend radius "often 12 to 15 times the diameter".
- Fault-current rule: full-load current × 100 ÷ impedance % (worst case, ignores
  source impedance). Examples: 1,000 kVA at 480 V = 1,203 A, 5% gives about
  24 kA; 750 kVA at 5.75% gives about 15.7 kA.
- Tap changer described as off-circuit, moved only de-energised; breather gel
  colours (blue to pink, orange to green) vary by maker.
- Exam bank: T1-11 asks for the smallest standard size for 380 kW at 0.8 and
  lists 500 kVA (380 ÷ 0.8 = 475 kVA, so 500 is right). T2-S03 gives 249 kVA
  (1.732 × 480 × 300 ÷ 1000 = 249.4). T2-E08: 520 ÷ 0.85 × 1.2 = 734 kVA
  and 902 A at 750 kVA are right.

## MEMMRD0872A Install and maintain electrical equipment

- Battery life "roughly halved for every 8 to 10 °C above design temperature"
  and sealed UPS batteries "often 3 to 5 years in warm rooms".
- Emergency lighting: monthly short test, yearly full-duration test, rated
  time "commonly 1 to 3 hours"; intervals are the maker's and the
  specification's.
- Generator: run "at set intervals, on load"; stale fuel and algae growth in
  tanks that are rarely used.
- Tariff figures (J$50 and J$55 per kWh) are examples only; GCT is mentioned
  only as "at the current rate".
- Pitted contacts: "never file them" is the kit's rule, following most makers;
  check it against the contactors the Centre uses.
- Exam bank: T1-41 (2 kW × 5 h × 30 days × J$60 = J$18,000) and T1-05
  (2,000 ÷ 80 hours a week = 25 weeks) are correct.

## MEMMRD0892A Install and maintain electronic electrical equipment and distribution circuits

- DC bus "about 1.4 × supply voltage"; drive losses "2 to 4%"; "capacitor life
  halves for each 10 °C"; soft starter limits "2 to 4 times full-load
  current" against 6 to 8 times for DOL.
- Screen termination: 360° at both ends is the general EMC rule; some makers
  say analogue control screens are earthed at one end only. The kit says to
  follow the manual.
- SPD lead length "ideally under half a metre"; type 1/2/3 staging described in
  general terms. Check against the SPDs the Centre uses.
- RCD test: a 30 mA RCD must trip between 15 and 30 mA on a ramp test (IEC
  style). The kit mentions that some drives need a type B RCD.
- Leakage per computer "a few mA" (1.5 and 2 mA used in examples).
- Solar: the kit says grid connection needs the power company's approval and
  inspection and does not name the regulator or licence; add the Centre's
  local requirements.
- Voltage-drop limit "about 3 to 5% depending on the code"; the case uses 3%.
- Exam bank: T1-04 says 80% speed needs "about 51%" of power (0.8³ = 0.512) and
  T1-24 gives 11 V of 220 V as 5%; both are right. T2-S11 (2,000 VA × 0.8 =
  1,600 W) is right.

## MEMPLN0034A Coordinate and manage commissioning processes

- Hold point and witness point definitions follow common contract practice.
  Check against the ITP form the Centre uses.
- Retransfer delay "commonly a few minutes" and cool-down "a few minutes";
  the case limit of 12 seconds to transfer and the 10-second example are invented.
- Defects liability period "often 12 months, fixed by the contract".
- Thermal survey after "some hours on load, preferably with a good share of
  the design load".
- Government inspection before JPS connects (GER); the kit does not name
  acts or fees.
- Exam bank: T1-21 (62, 48, 70 A, average 60 A) is right: the largest
  difference is 60 − 48 = 12 A, which is 20%. T1-45 (12 boards × 3 h = 36 h
  ÷ 7.5 = 4.8, so 5 days) is right.
  T2-S11 (18 × 40 min = 12 hours) is right.
- T1-17 (overload set to the nameplate 14.2 A) assumes the maker gives no other
  setting; the kit says the same.
