# B040 — facts for an instructor to double-check

Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 3 (QUAL-EIM-L3). Kits written 2026-11-07, status "to-check".

## MEMMRD0423A Diagnose and repair faults in electrical and electronic systems
- Insulation test: 500 V DC and "at least 1 MΩ" is the British (BS 7671) minimum; confirm the Centre's own pass figure.
- Typical figures stated as ranges, to confirm: DOL starting current 6–8 × full-load; copper resistance rise about 0.4% per °C; soldering iron tip 320–370 °C; tin-lead solder melts about 183–190 °C, lead-free about 217–221 °C.
- Capacitor charge of about 310 V DC on a 220 V supply (peak value), and the VSD "wait the time on the label" rule (kit gives 10 minutes only as the case example).
- Lost-neutral example (20 Ω / 80 Ω loads → 44 V / 176 V) and the claim that a break in JPS's own neutral is reported to and repaired by JPS.
- Resistor colour code, 104 = 100 nF code, ripple at 100 Hz (full-wave) on a 50 Hz supply.
- Terminal numbers (A1–A2, 95–96, 13–14, 15–16/15–18) are IEC conventions; US panels may be marked differently.
- Exam bank: T1-12 (replace a 30 µF capacitor "with one of the same value and voltage rating") is sound; T2-S07 says the "centrifugal switch" cuts out the start winding, correct for the motor type described but some compressor motors use a start relay instead.

## MEMMRD0443A Diagnose & repair faults in electrical equipment
- Insulation figures: PI of about 2 or more "generally good" (IEEE 43 style limits); portable equipment limits (earth continuity about 0.1 Ω plus cord; insulation at least 1 MΩ Class I and 2 MΩ Class II) are from UK PAT guidance, so use the code of practice the Centre follows.
- "Insulation resistance roughly halves for each 10 °C" rule of thumb; "a small voltage unbalance gives six to ten times the current unbalance".
- Star-delta overload setting 0.58 × full-load current when the relay is in the winding circuit; only true for that wiring.
- Battery life halving for each 8–10 °C above 25 °C; 12.7–12.9 V charged VRLA at rest.
- PCBs in old capacitors (before about 1980) and NEPA as the body for battery disposal rules: confirm the Jamaican authority and wording.
- Plug colours: US-style narrow blade = line, wide = neutral; brass/silver/green screws.
- Exam bank: T1-09 (1 100 turns, 220 V, 120 turns → 24 V) is correct; T1-25 (220 V, 1 500 W → 32 Ω) is correct (32.3 Ω); T2-S03 (12 V) and T2-S04 (12.1 Ω) are correct.

## MEMMRD0663A Perform testing and inspection of electrical installations
- Changed (owner's decision): the kit and exam bank now teach and test the Jamaican, NEC-based method, not the British one. BS 7671/IET, Zs/Ze tables, the 80% rule, C1/C2/C3/FI codes, the 500 V/1 MΩ table from BS 7671, 30 mA RCD times, ring finals, U0 and the UK mΩ/m figures are gone. Conductors are AWG with mm² equivalents; all worked sums use 110 V / 220 V and the cable data given in the kit (loop resistance of hot + equipment grounding conductor, e.g. 12 AWG pair 10.4 mΩ/m).
- Now taught and tested: inspection against the NEC and the approved drawings; GER inspection, licensed inspector's certificate, then JPS connection; continuity of grounding and bonding conductors; neutral-ground separation in sub-panels; insulation resistance (500 V, commonly 1 MΩ or more, follow the specification); polarity with a plug-in tester; a single ground rod needs a second electrode unless 25 Ω or less; GFCI (about 4–6 mA) and AFCI tests; voltage drop (3% branch, 5% total, informational); the effective ground-fault current path; checks before energising; test record and a three-level defect report (immediate hazard, must correct, advisory). 45 T1 questions: 34 touched (4 only for option length); 8 of 15 short answers and all 10 essays rewritten; both practicals reworded. Kit: sections, cases and all 20 questions rewritten.
- Instructor must still confirm: (1) which NEC edition the GER has adopted, since GFCI and AFCI areas, the 1.8 m / 2.4 m electrode figures and the motor overload range (115–125% of nameplate) depend on it; (2) the current GER procedure, forms, who may certify as a licensed inspector, and what an alteration (one extra outlet) needs; (3) the Centre's test limits: insulation test voltage and minimum, bonding resistance, the margin below the instant-trip limit, and the instant-trip current of the Centre's breakers (the kit uses 5 to 10 times rating, 10 times in the worked example; maker's curve rules), plus the inspection intervals the GER or insurers expect.
- Cable figures are typical copper at about 20 °C for same-size hot and grounding conductors; check them against the Centre's cable tables.

## MEMMRD0673A Coordinate the installation of electrical wiring support system infrastructure
- PVC expansion figure of about 0.06 mm per metre per °C and the worked example (54 mm for 30 m, 30 °C).
- Trunking space factor of 45% (the exam bank's figure; other codes use different percentages, and conduit fill differs).
- Typical tray support spacing "about 1.5 m": always from the maker's data and the specification.
- Bend radius "6 to 12 times cable diameter", conduit "two 90° bends between draw-in points".
- Coastal and hurricane advice, and the statement that an ordinary expanding anchor may not hold in hollow block.
- Worked costs use invented J$ prices (tray J$8 500 and J$7 800 per 3 m; brackets J$1 250–1 400); GCT left "at the current rate".
- Exam bank: T1-13 (75 × 50 mm at 45% → 1 688 mm²) is correct (1 687.5). T1-26 (46 m, 3 m lengths, 10% waste → 17) is correct (16.9). T1-45 (180 m, 3 × 12 m/day → 5 days) is correct. T2-E05 totals J$303 000 correctly.

## MEMMRD0683A Coordinate the installation of electrical cable and fixture
- Pulling tension of about 70 N per mm² of copper conductor (eye on conductors) is a common figure; always use the cable maker's value.
- Bending radius multiples (6–12 × overall diameter).
- Lighting: typical lux levels (corridor 100, restaurant 150–200, classroom 300, kitchen/desk 500), LED efficacy 80–150 lm/W, utilisation factor 0.4–0.7, maintenance factor about 0.8, CCT bands.
- Emergency lighting now follows the Jamaican code (NEC / NFPA 101), not BS 5266: average at least 1 foot-candle (about 10.8 lux) at the start, no point below 0.1 foot-candle at the start, and at the end of the 90 minutes an average of at least 0.6 foot-candle (about 6.5 lux) with no point below 0.06 foot-candle, 1.5 h (90 min) duration and battery, monthly 30-second and annual 90-minute tests with written records, exit signs, plus the Government Electrical Regulator inspecting; c4 battery changed from 3 h to 90 min; the "British terms" sentence and one exam stem word ("mains") replaced. Instructor must confirm: the figures against the NFPA 101 edition adopted in Jamaica (the published code also sets end-of-duration averages of 0.6 fc and minimum 0.06 fc, which the owner's 0.1 fc minimum does not mention), the record-keeping period, and who tests and inspects. Other IEC-style terms (Class I/II, protective conductor, line conductor, polarity, flex) remain; confirm the Centre's preferred NEC wording (equipment grounding conductor, ungrounded conductor, cord).
- Voltage-drop limit of 3% on a branch circuit (NEC recommendation, not a rule).
- Dimmer examples (20–150 W LED rating) are illustrative.
- Exam bank: T1-33 (64 m + 15% = 73.6 m → "about 74 m") is correct. T1-34 (90 ÷ 6 = 15 h) correct. T2-S05 (60 × 4 500 = J$270 000) correct. T2-E05 mentions "blades at least the height the maker and specification require" without a figure; fine.
