# Batch B004: what an instructor should check

Five Welding Level 2 kits, all `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by
maker or job are written as typical, with "follow the manufacturer's
instructions and your instructor". These are the points the writer was least
sure of, and the places where the exam bank needs a look.

## MEMFAB0051D Perform brazing and/or silver soldering

- Silver brazing joint gap "about 0.05–0.15 mm"; lap overlap "at least 3 × the thinner part".
- Melting ranges: silver alloys 600–800 °C, copper-phosphorus 650–800 °C, brass rod 870–900 °C; silver flux active about 550–800 °C and turns clear at about 600 °C.
- Air-LPG flame "about 1 900 °C"; oxygen cylinders "often 150–200 bar or more when full".
- Flame for brass rod on steel: the kit says many instructors use neutral to slightly oxidising (to cut zinc fuming). Confirm the Centre's practice.
- Acetylene cylinder delivered lying down: "stand it upright and wait the time the supplier gives" (no figure). Air-LPG shut-down: close the cylinder, let the flame burn the hose empty, then close the torch valve.
- Quenching: copper and brass parts may go into hot water after the filler sets; steel and mixed-metal joints never. Check this against the Centre's rule.
- Exam bank: practical 2 says "heat the steel to a dull red" for brass rod. Brass flows at about 870–900 °C, nearer cherry red, so the kit says "glows red (your instructor will show you the colour)".
- Exam bank: T1-20 ("brass (bronze) rod = copper and zinc") and T2-S14 ("bronze = copper and tin") are both right but read as contradictory. The kit explains both.

## MEMFAB0052C GMAW (MIG)

- Starting-point table for 0.8–0.9 mm wire with argon/CO2: 1–1.6 mm sheet about 15–17 V and 50–90 A; 3 mm about 17–19 V and 100–140 A; 6 mm about 19–22 V and 150–200 A.
- Spray transfer "above a transition current, often around 200 A", needing "about 80% argon or more"; short-circuit "about 20 to 200 times a second".
- Shades "about 10–11 for short-circuit work, 12–13 for spray"; stick-out 6–12 mm (about 10 mm); flow 10–20 L/min.
- Lap joint work angle "about 60–70° from the bottom sheet". This varies between textbooks.
- ER70S-6 versus -3, and the "-6 copes better with light scale and rust" claim; 70 000 psi given as about 480 MPa.
- Exam bank: T2-E06 aims the wire "slightly more at the bottom plate, because the pool tends to sag down onto it". The reason given is odd, because sag argues for aiming higher. The kit says "into the root or very slightly onto the bottom plate". The instructor should settle the teaching.
- Exam bank: practical 2 calls a lap laid on the bench a flat fillet (1F). Strictly that is a horizontal fillet (2F/PB). The kit points this out.

## MEMFAB0061C Manual heating and thermal cutting

- Cutting chart table (the least certain figures): 3–10 mm oxygen 1.5–2.5 bar and acetylene 0.15–0.3 bar; 10–25 mm oxygen 2–3.5 bar and acetylene 0.2–0.4 bar. These are marked as a guide only. Check them against the Centre's nozzle chart.
- Acetylene withdrawal limit "often about one-seventh of the contents per hour".
- Heat colour table (dull red about 600–700 °C, cherry about 750–800 °C, bright red to orange about 850–950 °C); ignition temperature "roughly 850–900 °C"; kerf "about 1.5–3 mm".
- Flame straightening at most a dull red, "about 600–650 °C"; blue heat "roughly 200–400 °C"; fire watch "often 30 to 60 minutes".
- Case c1: heating a wheel nut with the tyre fitted is treated as a tyre-burst hazard (wheel off first). Confirm this against the Centre's own guidance.
- The plasma shade is given only as "from the maker's chart"; no figure is stated.
- Exam bank: no errors found. Its acetylene limit (100 kPa / 15 psi) matches the MEMFAB0051D bank's "about 1 bar". Some European suppliers quote 1.5 bar.

## MEMFAB0062C GTAW (TIG), flat and horizontal

- Tungsten largest currents on DCEN: 1.0 mm about 80 A, 1.6 mm about 150 A, 2.4 mm about 250 A, 3.2 mm about 400 A. Case c2 relies on 95 A being too much for a 1.0 mm tungsten.
- Rules of thumb: about 30–40 A per mm for carbon steel; post-flow about 1 second per 10 A; tacks every 25–50 mm on 1.5–2 mm sheet; rod diameter about equal to the sheet thickness.
- Colour bands: ceriated grey (ISO) / orange (AWS), zirconiated white (ISO) / brown (AWS). The kit says lanthanated (gold) suits both AC and DC.
- Cup numbers in sixteenths of an inch (No. 6 about 9.5 mm, No. 8 about 12.7 mm); gas-lens stick-out "often about 10 mm".
- Stainless heat-tint table; fillers 4043/5356 for aluminium and 308L for 304.
- Exam bank: T2-E05 says "leaning in the direction of travel so that you push". This can be read as a drag angle. The kit says the tungsten points the way you travel.

## MEMFAB0071C Fabrication, forming, bending and shaping

- The neutral axis is taken at mid-thickness, as the exam bank does. In real tight bends it sits nearer the inside, and the kit says so.
- Guillotine: stainless "often about two-thirds of the mild steel capacity"; aviation snip handle colours (yellow straight, red left, green right) given as "often".
- Allowances: wired edge 2.5 × the wire diameter; grooved seam 3 × the seam width, split between the two sheets.
- Annealing aluminium with a "soap or marker test" (no temperature given); copper heated to a dull red, then cooled in water or air.
- Jamaica: corrugated roofing sheet called "zinc".
- Worked sums (checked twice): L-bracket 50/30 mm, 2 mm sheet, inside radius 4 mm gives about 76 mm; cylinder 400 mm ID in 5 mm plate gives about 1 272 mm; case c3 bracket gives 61.7 mm; case c2 chimney gives about 952 mm.
- Exam bank: no errors found. Practical 2's 100 mm radius template is the outside radius of the 200 mm OD cylinder.
