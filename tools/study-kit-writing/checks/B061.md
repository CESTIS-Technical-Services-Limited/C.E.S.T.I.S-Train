# B061 – facts for an instructor to double-check

All five kits are for HOSPITALITY VILLA/PROPERTIES SERVICES LEVEL 2. Tax rates, service charges and exchange rates in the worked sums are made up and labelled so in the kits; none is a real figure.

## THHFRO0012C Receive and process reservations
- High season is given as "about mid-December to April" (follow the property's own calendar). Confirm for the Centre's local market.
- Typical check-in "from about 2 or 3 p.m." and check-out "about 11 a.m. or noon" vary by property.
- Meal plans: BP (Bermuda Plan, full breakfast) and AP/FAP are included beyond the bank's EP, CP, MAP and all-inclusive. Confirm the Centre uses these terms.
- Worked example adds service charge first, then GCT on the subtotal; properties differ. The kit tells trainees to follow the property's rate sheet.
- Advice not to keep the card security code after authorisation follows general card-payment practice, not a Jamaican rule I could cite.
- Exam bank: T1-38 and the notes both use 75% occupancy; fine, but the same example appears in the exam and the notes.

## THHFRO0022D Provide accommodation reception services
- Walking an overbooked guest: kit says the property "usually pays for the room and transport"; policy differs by property.
- Emergency numbers: police 119; ambulance and fire 110. Confirm these are current.
- Passports: the kit says not to keep a guest's passport overnight unless law or policy requires it; check the Centre's local registration practice.
- Exam bank: T1-09 lists "voter ID card" as photo ID for Jamaicans; confirm what the Centre accepts (driver's licence, passport, national ID).

## THHFRO0091C Provide bell services
- Licensed public passenger vehicles carry red number plates, and tourist taxis are often linked to JUTA. Confirm both claims.
- "Heavy suitcases may be 20 to 30 kg; airlines allow about 23 kg" is a typical figure; confirm for the Centre.
- Many guests leave about 3 hours before an international flight (typical).
- Emergency numbers 119 (police) and 110 (ambulance, fire) as above.
- Exam bank: T2-S08 calls a guest who leaves without paying a "skipper"; this is front-office slang and may not be taught.
- Exam bank: T1-39 says the front desk or a licensed cambio can change money; confirm the Centre's guidance.

## THHFRO0101D Develop and apply conversational skills in a foreign language
- Have a Spanish, French and German speaker check all phrases, accents and the sound guides (approximate English respellings).
- Spanish "Su habitación es esta", "Enseguida lo arreglamos", "Que disfrute de su estancia" and "Que tenga una buena estancia" are my own additions beyond the bank.
- Greeting times (buenas tardes from about noon until dark; Guten Morgen until about 10 or 11) are conventions and vary by country.
- Many Spanish and Latin American guests may have two surnames; the kit says to use the first surname with the title.
- Jamaican visitor markets and resort ownership are mentioned in general terms only.
- Exam bank: T1-39 says "Bitte" can mean "please" and "you're welcome" (correct); T2-S02 accepts "buenas noches" for "Good night (or evening)". Neither is an error.

## THHFRO0112C Facilitate access to external services
- Drive times (Montego Bay to Negril about 1 to 1.5 hours; to Ocho Rios about 1.5 to 2 hours) are typical, not exact. Confirm locally.
- Hurricane season June to November.
- Car hire: a home licence or international permit and a credit card; Jamaican companies' rules vary.
- Emergency numbers 119 and 110 as above.
- Exam bank: T2-S05 expands TPDCo as "Tourism Product Development Company" (correct).
- Exam bank: T1-08 says Jamaicans drive on the left (correct).
