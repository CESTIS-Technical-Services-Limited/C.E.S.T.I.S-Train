# Batch B027: what an instructor should check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 2 kits, all
`"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by maker, standard or job are given
as typical, with "follow the maker's data and your instructor". The points
below are the ones the writer was least sure of, plus anything in the exam
banks that looks wrong. Every sum in the notes and questions was worked twice.

## EETOPT0152A Apply the fibre optic technology

- Loss figures: "maximum often used" 0.75 dB per connector pair, 0.3 dB per
  splice, multimode 3.5 dB/km (850 nm) and 1.5 dB/km (1300 nm), singlemode
  0.5 dB/km outdoors and 1.0 dB/km indoors (TIA-568 style). Typical good
  values: fusion splice "well under 0.1 dB", mechanical splice "about 0.1 to
  0.5 dB", connector pair "about 0.2 to 0.5 dB".
- Bend radius rule "20 × diameter while pulling, 10 × once installed";
  outdoor cable pulling force "often about 2,700 N (600 lbf)"; small indoor
  cables "a few hundred newtons". The data sheet always wins.
- Cleave angle "under about 1°" for fusion splicing; VFL "about 650 nm" and
  "up to a few kilometres"; IPA "99% or as the maker says".
- Colours: OS1/OS2 yellow, OM1/OM2 orange, OM3/OM4 aqua (OM4 violet in some
  countries), OM5 lime green; connector bodies beige or aqua (multimode), blue
  (singlemode UPC), green (APC, about 8°). LC 1.25 mm and SC 2.5 mm ferrules.
  "OS1 mostly indoors, OS2 mostly outdoors" is a simplification.
- Jamaica claims: fibre to the home in many towns; undersea cables "carry
  most of the island's international internet traffic"; fibre on JPS poles
  and in manholes installed only by authorised crews.
- Exam bank: no errors found. T2-S11 accepts "angle polished connector";
  the usual expansion is "angled physical contact" (the kit uses that).

## MEMCOR0012C Plan a complete work activity

- Contingency "about 10 to 20%" and waste allowance "about 10%" are general
  guidance; check against the Centre's practice.
- Jamaican supply units: conduit in 10 ft (about 3 m) lengths, building wire
  in 500 ft (about 152 m) rolls, sizes in AWG.
- Case c4 relies on the NEC rule that 14 AWG copper is protected at no more
  than 15 A (12 AWG for 20 A circuits).
- Dead test order taught as the exam bank gives it: protective (grounding)
  conductor continuity, insulation resistance, polarity; live checks only
  afterwards by a competent person. Confirm against the Centre's procedure.
- Inspection by a licensed electrical inspector under the GER before JPS
  connects (same wording as the pilot EEMELS0011B kit).
- Old fluorescent tubes treated as hazardous waste (mercury); confirm the
  Centre's disposal route. The 3 × 3 risk matrix is illustrative.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0071E Use electrical/electronic measuring devices

- CAT categories: IV origin, III fixed installation and panels, II plug-in
  equipment, I (now "O" in newer IEC 61010 editions) non-mains circuits.
  Probe tips "often 4 mm or less" follows UK GS38 guidance.
- Insulation testing: "500 V DC is common" for 110/220 V circuits and "many
  test standards accept no less than about 1 MΩ" (BS 7671 style; the NEC sets
  no field-test figure). The kit tells trainees the instructor gives the test
  voltage and pass value: please confirm the Centre's figures.
- Continuity beeper threshold "often a few tens of ohms"; 12 V lead-acid
  battery "12.6–12.8 V charged, about 12.0 V or less well run down"; a cold
  filament "often ten times or more" lower in resistance than when lit.
- Outlet polarity for US-type outlets: narrow slot (brass screw) line, wide
  slot (silver) neutral, round or D hole (green) ground; screw lamp holder
  centre contact to switched line.
- Case c2 (water heater on a two-pole GFCI breaker, element 16.2 Ω,
  insulation 0.02 MΩ to the tank) is a constructed example.
- Calibration "often every year, as the Centre's schedule says".
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0132C Use industrial instrumentation measuring devices

- Conversions: 1 psi ≈ 6.9 kPa, 1 bar ≈ 14.5 psi, atmospheric about 101 kPa;
  water about 9.8 kPa per metre; 1 m³/h ≈ 16.7 L/min.
- Gauge range rule: full scale "about twice the normal working pressure",
  working pressure "not above about three-quarters of the scale".
- Pt100: 100 Ω at 0 °C, about 0.385 Ω/°C, 138.5 Ω at 100 °C. Infrared:
  12:1 spot example; shiny metal "usually reads far too low".
- Jamaica: water boils "about 98 °C in Mandeville", lower in the Blue
  Mountains. Motors on 50 Hz: about 2,900 rpm (2-pole) and 1,450 rpm (4-pole).
- Calibration "often every 6 or 12 months"; reference instruments calibrated
  by an accredited laboratory (no Jamaican body is named). The ±1.6% of full
  scale tolerance and the dairy and compressor figures are examples.
- 4–20 mA: "well below 4 mA or above 20 mA point to a fault" (no NAMUR
  figures given). Magnetic flow meters "not for oils or fuels"; rotameter
  read "at the point on the float that the maker marks".
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0152C Use graphical techniques and perform simple statistical computations (Basic)

- All data sets are invented; sums, medians, angles and percentages were
  checked twice (pie example 126 + 90 + 72 + 45 + 27 = 360°; c5 corrected
  mean 16.12 Ω, uncorrected 30.6 Ω).
- Spreadsheet function names (AVERAGE, MEDIAN, MODE, MIN, MAX, COUNT) are
  given as found in most spreadsheets, with no product named.
- Line graphs may start the vertical axis near the lowest value with a
  marked break; bar charts must start at zero (used in P-11 and P-20).
- Case c4 says energy use rises on hot days "probably because"
  air-conditioning works harder; the notes stress that a link is not proof
  of a cause.
- Exam bank: no errors found.
