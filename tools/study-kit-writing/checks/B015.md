# B015: facts for an instructor to check

Five Welding Level 3 kits in `study-kits/welding-fabrication-level-3/`. All are
`"status": "to-check"`. Figures below are given in the kits as "typical" or
"about", with "follow the maker / the WPS / your instructor"; they are the
ones the writer was least sure of.

## MEMCOR0191B Use hand tools

- Tap names follow the exam bank's British usage: taper, second, plug
  (bottoming). American books call the *second* tap the "plug" tap; trainees
  using US catalogues may be confused.
- Typical figures: hacksaw 40–60 strokes a minute; TPI bands (14 / 18 / 24 /
  32); prick punch about 30°, centre punch about 90°; cold chisel about 60°;
  die rod for M10 about 9.8–9.9 mm; solid rivet tail about 1.5 × diameter for
  a snap head; grinder tool rest 3 mm or less.
- Snips: "often about 1.2 mm mild steel" capacity; case c4 rates the team's
  snips at 0.7 mm stainless. Check against the Centre's own snips.
- Copper-beryllium tools: the kit says never regrind them in the workshop
  (toxic dust) and send them to a supplier. Check the Centre's practice for
  aluminium-bronze tools.
- Torque wrench: calibration "often every 12 months", avoid the bottom fifth
  of the scale; 1 ft·lb ≈ 1.36 N·m.
- Insulated tools described as rated 1000 V with the double-triangle mark.

## MEMFAB0013B Monitor quality of production welding/fabrication

- Control-chart signals are given as "about seven points on one side" and
  "about six or seven rising or falling". Rule sets differ (8 or 9 in some);
  the kit tells trainees to follow their control plan.
- Capability: Cp = tolerance ÷ 6 standard deviations, "1.33 or more" as room
  to spare. Exam bank T1-37 calls a 6.2–7.8 mm spread in a 6–8 mm tolerance
  "only just fits" (Cp about 1.25): instructors may want to agree the wording.
- Hot-dip galvanising "at about 450 °C"; vent/drain holes; silicone
  anti-spatter and oil-based markers leave bare spots.
- Timed wire run-out example uses 6 seconds.
- All sums (rates, J$ costs, payback) were worked twice; worth one more check.

## MEMFAB0022B Advanced manual thermal cutting, gouging and shaping

- Air carbon arc current by electrode size (4.0 mm 90–150 A up to 9.5 mm
  350–600 A, DCEP) is from memory of maker charts: check against the
  Centre's electrode maker.
- Typical ACAG figures: air 550–690 kPa (80–100 psi); stick-out up to about
  150 mm, reset at 50–75 mm; electrode angle about 35–45°; noise often over
  100 dB(A); filter about shade 12 up to about 500 A.
- Oxy-fuel gouging nozzle angle "often about 15–30°" and the steeper
  heating angle at the start: check against the nozzle maker's guide.
- Acetylene "about 1 bar (15 psi)" limit (UK guidance often quotes 1.5 bar);
  withdrawal one-seventh per hour (some suppliers say one-tenth). Shut-down
  order given as "fuel gas first in many workshops": check the Centre's rule.
- Plasma open-circuit voltage "often over 250 V".
- Set-in branch: kit says hole = branch outside diameter, cut with the torch
  parallel to the branch centre line (case c4, P-12). The exam essay covers
  only a set-on branch (torch pointing at the main's centre). Please confirm.
- Defect excavation goes past each crack end "as the repair procedure says";
  no figure is given (AWS repair practice often uses 50 mm).
- Jamaica: daytime sea breeze blows inland; dry months and bush fires;
  110 for fire and ambulance.

## MEMFAB0023B Advanced GMAW (MIG)

- Typical figures: spray transition "often about 200 A or more" for
  1.0–1.2 mm steel wire; short-circuit 15–22 V, spray 24–32 V; stick-out
  10–15 mm (short-circuit), 15–25 mm (spray); gas 12–20 L/min; wind limit
  about 8 km/h; filter shade 11–14.
- Wire mass: 1.0 mm about 6.2 g/m, 1.2 mm about 8.9 g/m (used in case c1).
- The kit says short-circuit MIG is not prequalified under the AWS structural
  code and needs a qualified procedure (no clause number given). Confirm
  against the code the Centre uses.
- Welder qualification "often at least once every six months" use.
- Aluminium conducts heat "three to four times" as fast as steel and expands
  about twice as much; stainless conducts about a third as well and expands
  about half as much again; stainless interpass "often about 150 °C".
- ER5356 gives a closer colour match after anodising than ER4043 (case c2).

## MEMFAB0033B Advanced GTAW (TIG)

- DCEN heat split "about two-thirds to the work" is the traditional teaching
  figure.
- Typical figures: AC balance start 65–75% EN; post-flow about 1 s per 10 A;
  air-cooled torches up to about 150–200 A; gas 7–15 L/min; stick-out 3–6 mm
  (10–15 mm with a gas lens); filter shade about 10–13.
- Tungsten colours are given both ways (ISO grey/white, AWS orange/brown for
  ceriated/zirconiated). DCEN current by diameter (1.0 mm to about 80 A …
  3.2 mm 250–400 A) is typical only.
- Argon-hydrogen (up to about 5%) is said to be for austenitic stainless and
  nickel alloys only. Purge oxygen limit is left to the WPS (no figure).
- 5083 with ER4043 described as forming a brittle compound; ER5356 or ER5183
  recommended (case c2).
- Pickling paste: HF burns may not hurt at first; calcium gluconate gel "if
  the workplace keeps it". Check against the SDS of the paste the Centre uses.
- Jamaica: grade 316 recommended over 304 for seaside work.
