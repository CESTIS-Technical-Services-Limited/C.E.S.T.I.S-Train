# Batch B059: what an instructor should check

All five kits are `"status": "to-check"` (Hospitality Villa/Properties Services Level 2). Times, temperatures and sizes are given as typical figures with "follow the Centre's recipe cards and food safety plan". These are the points the writer was least sure of.

Points common to all five kits:

- Core cooking temperature 74 °C, danger zone 5 °C to 63 °C, hot-holding 63 °C or above, fridge 5 °C or below, freezer -18 °C or below (as in the banks). Some authorities use 70 °C held for 2 minutes, 4 °C to 60 °C, or 8 °C for the fridge. Confirm the figures the Centre teaches.
- Cooling guide "to about 21 °C within 2 hours, then to 5 °C within a further 4 hours" (a common international guide, not in the banks). Confirm the Centre's rule.
- Chopping-board colour code (red raw meat, blue raw fish, yellow cooked meat, green salad and fruit, brown root vegetables, white bakery and dairy). Kits say "follow your Centre's code".
- Food Handler's Permit from the health department is stated as a legal requirement for food handlers (as in the bank). Confirm wording.
- Emergency numbers (ambulance and fire 110, police 119) used in two kits.

## THHCFP0321D Prepare and cook poultry dishes

- Bank and kit say "do not rinse raw chicken", but many Jamaican kitchens wash chicken with lime or vinegar. The kit tells trainees to follow the Centre's rule. Confirm which the Centre teaches.
- Thawing guide: whole 1.5 kg bird about 24 hours or more in the fridge; pieces 12 to 24 hours.
- Roast time for 1.5 kg: about 1¼ to 1½ hours at 180 to 190 °C (about 20 minutes per 500 g plus 20 minutes); jerk quarters 40 to 60 minutes; brown stew and curry 30 to 40 minutes; boiling fowl 2 to 3 hours.
- Deep-fry oil 160 to 180 °C (the bank says 160 to 180 °C; some fryer makers say 175 to 190 °C).
- "8 pieces" is taken as 2 drumsticks, 2 thighs, 2 wings, 2 breast pieces (the bank's wording is slightly different in its essay answer and multiple-choice question: breast halves versus whole breasts cut in two).
- The kit mentions a separate board colour for raw poultry in some kitchens; the bank's colour code does not give one.

## THHCFP0331D Prepare and cook meat and seafood

- Bank T2-S06 defines mutton as sheep meat over about a year old; common definitions say over 1 to 2 years. The kit notes that in Jamaica "mutton" often means goat meat.
- Whole beef steaks may be served rare or medium by guest choice (kit warns vulnerable groups). Confirm the Centre's policy.
- Fish core temperature about 63 °C (the bank says only opaque and flaking); UK guidance is 70 °C for 2 minutes.
- Cooking times: curry goat 1½ to 2½ hours (30 to 45 minutes in a pressure cooker); oxtail 2½ to 3½ hours (45 to 60 minutes pressure cooker); pepper shrimp 3 to 5 minutes; frying fish 170 to 180 °C.
- Histamine (tuna, mackerel, mahi-mahi, kingfish) and ciguatera (large reef fish such as barracuda) as described; confirm which fish the Centre names for local advice. The bank says tuna and mackerel for histamine and barracuda for the toxin, as the kit does.
- Lobster and conch closed seasons mentioned only in general terms; no dates given.
- Pawpaw (papain) as a tenderiser, and wrapping meat in pawpaw leaves, is stated as a Jamaican practice.
- Pressure cooker fill level "two-thirds at most, half for beans" is the usual maker's guide; the maker's manual decides.
- Bank T1-34 gives blue as the colour for raw fish and T1-40 of unit THHCFP1031B gives red for raw meat; they agree with the kit's code.

## THHCFP0651D Prepare vegetables, fruits, eggs and farinaceous dishes

- Ackee: only fully ripe ackee from pods opened naturally; seeds and the pink-red membrane removed; boil and discard the water. Hypoglycin A named as the toxin. Confirm the Centre's wording and the approved-producer point for canned ackee.
- Red kidney beans: soak at least 5 hours or overnight, discard the water, boil hard for at least 10 minutes. The bank says "soak, then boil hard in fresh water"; the 10 minutes is the standard food safety figure.
- Bammy: the kit says it is made from bitter cassava that has been grated and pressed to remove poisonous juice (as is traditional).
- Egg times (soft 3 to 4, medium 5 to 6, hard 10 to 12 minutes; poach 3 to 4 minutes) are for a medium egg and vary.
- Pasta: about 1 litre of water per 100 g (the bank's figure; many chefs use less); portions 60 to 80 g (starter), 100 g or more (main).
- Rice: water ratio 1 part rice to 1.5 to 2 parts water by volume varies with the rice; rice cooling "within 1 to 2 hours" and shelf life (often 1 day) are guides.
- Fried food oil temperature 160 to 180 °C; roasting vegetables 180 to 220 °C; macaroni pie 180 °C.
- Names of yams ("yellow, negro, white") are given as examples.

## THHCFP0671C Prepare stocks, sauces and soups

- Chicken stock time: the bank says about 2 to 3 hours; the kit says about 2 to 4 hours (many texts say 3 to 4). Brown stock 4 to 8 hours; fish stock 20 to 30 minutes; vegetable stock 30 to 45 minutes.
- Mirepoix about 1 part to 10 parts of water by weight, and 2 to 3 litres of water per kilogram of bones, are common guides, not exact.
- Béchamel: 50 to 60 g each of butter and flour per litre of milk for a medium sauce; hollandaise about 3 yolks to 200 to 250 g butter.
- Roux cooking times (white about 2 minutes, blond 4 to 5, brown 10 or more) are approximate.
- Five mother sauces (béchamel, velouté, espagnole, tomato, hollandaise) as in the bank; some texts count four (without hollandaise) or give a different fifth.
- Hollandaise: "kept warm, not hot, for a short time only". No hold time or temperature is given; the bank gives none either. Confirm the Centre's rule.
- Red peas soup toxin rule: same as above; the kit also repeats the rule for gungo peas and other dried pulses.
- Jamaican soup descriptions (fish tea, mannish water, pepperpot, Saturday soup) are short summaries; recipes vary by cook and by parish. Pepperpot soup here is the callaloo and okra Jamaican soup (the bank's meaning), not the Guyanese stew.
- Blender fill "no more than half or two-thirds" and "cool slightly" are general safety advice; the maker's manual decides.
- The bank's T1-04 option "so the bones stay white" is a plausible wrong answer; the kit gives the usual reason (slow release of scum and flavour).

## THHCFP1031B Use knives for basic task in the kitchen environment

- Classical cut sizes vary by book. Kit: julienne 2 × 2 × 40 mm (fine 1 mm); batonnet 6 × 6 × 50 mm; jardinière 4 × 4 × 20 mm; paysanne 1 cm × 1 cm × 2 mm; brunoise 3 mm (fine 1.5 mm); macédoine 5 mm; large, medium, small dice 20, 12, 6 mm. The bank says brunoise 2 to 3 mm, macédoine about 5 mm, julienne "2 to 3 mm", paysanne about 1 cm. The kit tells trainees to use the assessor's size card.
- Honing and sharpening angle 15 to 20 degrees (as in the bank); some Japanese knives use about 10 to 15 degrees.
- Pinch grip is taught as the knife grip; the bank lists it only as a short answer.
- Bank T1-21 says wipe a blade "from the spine towards the edge"; the kit follows it, with the edge turned away from the hand.
- Tomato blanching 10 to 15 seconds.
- Cut-resistant glove "on the hand that holds the food" (as in the bank).
- First aid for cuts, scald, and eye rinse follow general first aid; confirm against the Centre's first aid policy. The blue plaster and glove practice is as in the bank.
- Wooden boards: the kit says some kitchens use them if scrubbed and dried; confirm the Centre's rule.
