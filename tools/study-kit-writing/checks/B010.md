# B010: what an instructor should check

Welding Level 3 (MEM30215). All five kits are `"status": "to-check"`. Every sum in
the kits and their practice questions was worked twice. Prices, deposits,
warranties and contingencies in the examples are made up for teaching.

## MEMCOM0023B Perform internal and external customer service

- Stainless steel grades: the kit says 316 is "usually advised" near the sea or
  a pool, and 304 for most indoor and kitchen work.
- Galvaniser turnaround "often about a week". Hollow sections need vent and
  drain holes "as the galvaniser advises".
- Jamaican claim: "Fire officers in Jamaica regularly warn that fixed burglar
  bars trap people in house fires." The kit advises an escape grille in each
  bedroom.
- Unsafe requests: LPG cylinders go back to the gas supplier; forklift forks,
  hooks and chains are not repaired by welding; a cracked wheel rim is replaced.
  Check these against the Centre's safety policy.
- Public holidays named in a tip: Labour Day (May), Emancipation Day and
  Independence Day (August).
- The data protection law is named only by its purpose.
- Exam bank: no errors found. T1-43 counts "48 hours" as clock hours
  (Monday 2 p.m. to Wednesday 2 p.m.). The kit explains the difference between
  clock hours and working days.

## MEMCOR0012B Plan a complete activity

- Hot-work permit figures: anything that burns cleared "within about 10 m";
  fire watch "often 30–60 minutes" or as the permit says.
- Contingency "often somewhere between 5% and 15% of the direct cost".
- Jamaican claims: the wettest months are often May to June and September to
  November; the hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November. Many rural
  sites have no supply that can run a welding machine.
- Witness point: if the client was properly notified and does not attend, the
  work may go on. Check this against the ITPs the Centre uses.
- Exam bank: no errors found. T2-E02 is right if task E may start on day 1
  (float 7 days).

## MEMCOR0013B Assist in the provision of on the job training

- Roles: "NCTVET awards the NVQ-J"; "a qualified assessor judges competence; the
  workplace trainer does not sign anyone off". Check what the Centre's workplace
  trainers sign on NVQ-J records.
- Vertical-up fillet (3F) with MMAW: current "often about 10–20%" lower than in
  the horizontal position; electrode halfway between the plates and tilted
  "about 5–15°" upwards. Case figures: E7018 3.2 mm, WPS range 90–115 A.
- TIG set-up for mild steel: DC, electrode negative; tungsten ground lengthwise.
- Gas sets: the kit says to light and shut down "in the order the maker and the
  Centre's procedure give". It does not say whether fuel gas or oxygen is shut
  first, because makers' instructions differ.
- First aid: cool a burn for about 20 minutes; rinse a speck from the eye with
  clean water or eyewash.
- Learning styles: the kit teaches visual, auditory and kinaesthetic, but
  stresses that most trainees learn best from a mix. The exam's T2-S13 expects
  "auditory".
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0022B Perform related computations

- Deposition efficiency (typical): MMAW about 55–65%, solid MIG wire about
  90–95%, flux-cored wire about 80–85%. The exam bank uses 60% for MMAW.
- Heat input: the kit uses the bank's formula, and notes that some codes
  multiply by a thermal efficiency factor for the process.
- Disc speeds: a 230 mm disc on a 6 600 rev/min grinder gives about 79.5 m/s,
  so it needs an 80 m/s disc; the example in the notes is a 125 mm disc at
  11 000 rev/min, about 72 m/s.
- Jamaican claim: paint is often sold by the US gallon (about 3.8 L) and the
  quart (about 0.95 L).
- Exam bank: T2-E07's model answer says the nearest metric plate to ½ inch
  (12.7 mm) is 12 mm. Half-inch plate is often stocked in Jamaica, and any
  change of size needs approval, as the kit says. No arithmetic errors found.

## MEMCOR0023C Write technical reports (advanced)

- **Exam bank error, T1-33** ("Why should a report name the edition or revision
  of a standard or drawing it refers to?"): none of the four options is right.
  The keyed answer is option 1, "So the report looks more official and more
  costly to the customer", which is wrong. The right answer, which the kit
  teaches, is "so the reader knows exactly which requirements applied". Write
  a correct option and key it.
- Version numbering: the kit teaches v0.x for drafts, v1.0 for the first issue
  and a change record. Check this against the Centre's document control.
- NCR dispositions: rework, repair, use as is (a concession, only with the
  client's written agreement), and scrap or return to supplier. The kit also
  separates "correction" from "corrective action".
- The data protection law is named only by its purpose.
