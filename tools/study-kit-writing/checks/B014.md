# B014: what an instructor should check

Welding Level 3 (MEM30215) core units. All five kits are `"status": "to-check"`.
People, businesses and jobs in the case studies are made up; no real company is
named. Prices in J$ are invented examples, always "before GCT".

## MEMCOR0122C Write technical reports (basic)

- Dates: the kit says Jamaica normally writes the day first (03/11/26 = 3 November)
  and American forms the month first, so it recommends writing the month in words.
- Worked example: 8, 3, 2, 1 defects = 57%, 21%, 14%, 7% (rounded, adds to 99%);
  9 of 60 welds = 15%. Practice: 12 of 160 welds = 7.5%.
- NCR dispositions taught as rework, repair, use as is (with a written concession)
  or scrap. Check these match the Centre's NCR form.
- Record retention is left to "the contract, the company's rules or the law"; no
  number of years is given.
- Incident reports: "follow the data protection law" (no section quoted).
- Exam bank: T2-S06 accepts "label" as well as "caption"; decide if that is wanted.

## MEMCOR0131B Undertake interactive workplace communication

- Jamaican Creole: the kit says use whatever spoken language gets the message
  across, but write records in Standard English. Practice question P-13 accepts a
  trainee's correct explanation given partly in Patois. Confirm the Centre agrees.
- Radio words taught: over, out (never "over and out"), say again, received/roger,
  stand by, "stop, stop, stop". Check against the procedure the Centre uses.
- SDS sections named: 2 hazards, 4 first aid, 5 fire-fighting, 7 handling and
  storage, 8 exposure controls/PPE, 13 disposal (16-section GHS format).
- Safety sign shapes and colours follow ISO 7010.
- Exam bank T1-34: the key (a falling reject rate means better welding) is fine,
  but the kit also tells trainees to ask what is counted and over what period.

## MEMCOR0141B Follow principles of OH&S in work environment

- Jamaican law: the kit names the Factories Act and the Ministry of Labour and
  Social Security's Occupational Safety and Health Department, and says a broader
  OSH law "has been in preparation". Confirm which law is in force now, and the
  rule for reporting serious accidents.
- Emergency numbers: 110 fire and ambulance, 119 police.
- Risk matrix bands (1–4 low, 5–10 medium, 12–16 high, 20–25 very high) are
  described as typical, not a standard. Practice P-08 relies on 12 = high.
- Confined spaces: oxygen entry range about 19.5% to 23.5% (US OSHA figures).
- Oxygen kept apart from fuel gases "often about 6 m" or by a fire-resisting wall
  (US OSHA; other guidance differs).
- Flashback: "close the torch valves at once (most makers say the oxygen valve
  first), then the cylinder valves". Check with the maker of the Centre's torches.
  Acetylene that has lain down: "wait the time the supplier gives" (no figure).
- First aid: burns cooled about 20 minutes; chemical eye splash at least 15
  minutes (the bank says 15; some guides say 20). Heat: a cup of water every
  15–20 minutes during hard work (US NIOSH/OSHA guide).
- Noise: grinder about 95–105 dB(A); "every 3 dB doubles the energy" (US OSHA
  uses a 5 dB rule for its legal limit).
- Extinguisher classes: US letters (A, B, C electrical, D, K) and European (C
  gases, F cooking oils) both mentioned.
- Welding fume classed as a cause of cancer by IARC (2017). Pickling paste often
  contains hydrofluoric acid (special first aid named on its SDS).
- Hot-work lanyards "for example with a steel wire core".
- Exam bank: T1-21's key says the respirator is "fit-checked"; tight-fitting
  respirators also need a fit test (the kit teaches both). T1-23 gives 120 kg for
  a 2 m length of 10 mm plate without its width (it implies about 0.76 m wide).

## MEMCOR0161B Plan to undertake a routine task

- Calculations to re-work: uprights 3 lengths (3 per 6 m length), rails 4 lengths
  (5 per length, different angle size, so offcuts are not shared), cost J$68,200;
  24 h + 15% = 27.6 h; 6 of 18 grills = 33% behind; 26 h against 20 h = 30% over;
  diagonals 1,500 × 800 → 1,700 mm and 2,400 × 1,000 → 2,600 mm; worked example
  grill J$56,000 before GCT; float of task B = 3 h.
- Typical figures given as guides: kerf about 2–3 mm (abrasive disc), waste
  allowance 5–15%, contingency 10–15%.
- Jamaica: rainy seasons "usually around May to June and September to November";
  hurricane season 1 June to 30 November.
- Hold point / witness point / inspection point definitions; check they match the
  Centre's inspection and test plan wording.
- No errors found in the exam bank.

## MEMCOR0171B Use and maintain graduated measuring devices

- Expansion: steel about 0.012 mm per metre per °C (as the bank), stainless about
  0.017, aluminium about 0.023. Practice: 12 m × 15 °C × 0.012 ≈ 2.2 mm.
- The kit teaches that a steel tape and steel work at the same temperature grow
  together, so the reading is close to the 20 °C length (practice P-14).
- Tape accuracy classes (OIML/EC): Class I ±(0.1 + 0.1 L) mm, Class II
  ±(0.3 + 0.2 L) mm, L in whole metres; 12 m gives ±1.3 mm and ±2.7 mm (P-16).
- Micrometer check sizes 5.1, 10.3, 15.0, 20.2, 25.0 mm; gauge-block example
  41.37 mm = 1.37 + 40 (or 15 + 25), depending on the set.
- Bridge cam gauge: listed as measuring preparation angle, cap height, undercut
  and pitting depth, fillet throat and leg, and misalignment. Hi-lo gauge: "some
  models also measure the root gap and wall thickness".
- Fillet sizes: AWS drawings give the leg; ISO drawings mark throat "a" and leg
  "z"; throat about 0.7 × leg.
- Temperature crayon: dry chalky mark below the rating, melts at or above it;
  infrared thermometers read low on bright steel.
- Depth micrometer described only as "numbered the opposite way"; lever-type
  indicators said to read high at an angle (cosine error).
- Accreditation body named as JANAAC (Jamaica National Agency for Accreditation).
- No errors found in the exam bank.
