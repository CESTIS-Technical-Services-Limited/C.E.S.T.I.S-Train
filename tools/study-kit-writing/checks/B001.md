# Batch B001: what an instructor should check

Five Welding Level 2 kits, all `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by
maker or job are written as typical, with "follow the maker's instructions
and your instructor". The points below are the ones the writer was least
sure of, plus anything in the exam banks that looks wrong.

## BCGCOR0051D Use hand and power tools

- Hacksaw blade table (14 TPI coarse/thick soft sections, 18 general, 24 thin
  angle and tube, 32 thin sheet and thin-walled tube) and "roughly 40 to 60
  strokes a minute".
- Aviation snips "often about 1.2 mm mild steel"; handle colours said to vary
  by maker.
- Small angle grinders "around 10 000 to 12 000 rpm"; new-disc test run
  "about a minute"; resin-bonded discs carry a use-by date on the metal ring.
- Bench grinder tool rest "no more than about 3 mm" from the wheel.
- Pilot hole "4 to 5 mm"; ball pein example "0.45 kg".
- Noise rule of thumb: having to shout at "a metre or two".
- Jamaica: JPS 110 V and 220 V, 50 Hz; "ordinary outlets are usually 110 V".
  Advice that a portable generator is set up by a competent person, outdoors.
- Bending flat bar in the vice (bend line level with the top of the jaws) was
  added for practical 1; check it matches how the Centre teaches it.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0012D Plan a complete work activity

- All J$ prices are examples (SHS J$ 8 500 and J$ 8 900 per 6 m length,
  electrodes, discs, paint). GCT is mentioned only as "at the current rate".
- Steel weight rule: 7.85 kg per m² per mm of thickness; 6 m of
  40 × 40 × 3 SHS "about 20 kg" (about 3.5 kg/m without corner radii).
- Kerf "about 1 to 3 mm"; fire watch on a permit "often 30 to 60 minutes".
- Safety sign shapes and colours follow ISO 7010 (red prohibition, blue
  mandatory, yellow warning, green safe condition, red fire equipment).
- The workbench bar chart and cut plan (two 6 m lengths, offcuts 115 mm and
  369 mm with 3 mm cuts) were worked twice; re-check if the Centre changes
  them.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0042C Interpret standard specifications and manuals

- Bureau of Standards Jamaica described as the national body that publishes
  Jamaican standards and adopts international ones (regulatory work now sits
  with other bodies; the kit does not name them).
- The rating plate (U1 220 V, I1max 31 A / I1eff 18 A, U0 65 V, 35/60/100% at
  180/135/105 A, U2 = 20 + 0.04 × I2, IP23S) and the electrode table
  (60–90, 90–130, 130–180 A) are illustrative, not from a real machine.
- ISO process numbers: 111 MMAW, 135 MAG solid wire, 136 flux-cored with gas,
  141 TIG, 311 oxy-acetylene.
- Conversion 1 cfh ≈ 0.47 L/min. Signal words follow the ANSI Z535 style
  (DANGER will, WARNING could, CAUTION minor, NOTICE property).
- Welding symbols: AWS arrow side below the line; ISO arrow side on the solid
  line, other side on the dashed line; ISO "a" = throat, "z" = leg.
- Duty cycle measured at "often 40 °C".
- Exam bank: T1-02's correct option is cut short ("An agreed set of rules for
  how work or products must be"); it probably should end "...must be made or
  done".

## MEMCOR0081E Use marking out tools (shared with Electrical Installation)

- Prick punch point "often about 30°" (references give 30° to 60°); centre
  punch 90°.
- Kerf "about 1 mm for a hacksaw, a few millimetres for a cutting disc or
  plasma, more for gas cutting".
- "Some coloured chalks stain permanently" (red and fluorescent chalk).
- "Cables usually run straight up, down or across from switches, outlets and
  fittings" is general guidance. Check it against what the Electrical
  Installation programme teaches under the code used in Jamaica.
- Heights in the cases (450 mm and 1200 mm AFFL) are job values, not rules.
- The kit is written for both programmes and will be copied to
  electrical-installation; the overview names both and no qualification code.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0091D Draw and interpret sketches and simple drawings (shared with Electrical Installation)

- Projection symbol: the kit says the circles are to the right of the cone in
  first angle and to the left in third angle. Online sources disagree, so
  confirm against the Centre's drawing standard or textbook.
- Fold (bend) lines on developments: the kit follows practical 2 (thin chain
  lines) but says to check the drawing's notes, because some standards draw
  bend lines as thin continuous lines.
- Dimension figures "readable from the bottom or the right-hand side" (the
  aligned system); some drawings use horizontal-only figures.
- "British and European drawings often use first angle, American drawings
  third angle"; "drawings in Jamaica can be either".
- Switching lines on electrical layout plans (thin curved dashed lines from a
  switch to its lights): check that they match the Centre's sample drawings.
- Cabinet oblique at half depth; pencils 2H for construction and HB for
  outlines.
- Exam bank: practical 2 asks for fold lines "drawn as chain lines", which is
  one convention, not the only one (see above).
