# Batch B017: what an instructor should check

All five kits are `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by code, maker or
job are given as typical, with "follow the WPS / the maker's chart / the code
the job uses". These are the points the writer was least sure of.

## Exam bank errors found

- **MEMFAB0062B, T1-39** (hydrostatic test): the marked answer, "To cool the
  weld quickly so that it ends up harder and stronger", is wrong. Two options
  are near-duplicates ("cool the weld quickly…") and the right answer (to prove
  the pipe holds its test pressure without leaking) is missing. Fix the bank
  before it is used. The kit teaches the correct purpose.
- No arithmetic errors found in the other four banks (all sums re-worked).

## MEMFAB0053B Perform welding supervision

- Qualification rules are given as "typical, check the code": a test often
  covers up to about twice the test thickness; thick-plate tests (often 25 mm
  or more) may cover any thickness; a 6G pipe test "usually" covers most plate
  and pipe positions; E7018 tests "usually" cover easier electrode groups.
- Continuity: "often six months" without use of the process (AWS and ASME
  practice). Confirm for the code the Centre uses.
- P-04: a 6G MMAW-only welder filling and capping behind another welder's GTAW
  root, with both IDs on the weld map. Most codes allow it; confirm for the
  Centre's code and WPSs.
- Witness point vs hold point (P-16): work may go on after proper notice if
  the inspector does not attend a witness point. Check this matches the ITPs
  the Centre uses.
- Fire watch "often 30–60 minutes"; combustibles cleared "at least 10 m".
- Operator factor "often about 20–40%" for manual welding.
- Legal duty described only as "Jamaica's workplace safety law"; no Act or
  section named. Emergency number 110 (fire and ambulance).

## MEMFAB0061B Perform manual heating and thermal cutting

- Ignition temperature "roughly 870–900 °C"; flame temperatures acetylene about
  3 100 °C, propane about 2 800 °C; aluminium oxide melts about 2 050 °C.
- Oxygen cylinder "often about 150–200 bar"; oxygen kept from fuel gas "often
  at least about 6 m or behind a fire-resistant wall".
- Acetylene draw-off "about one-seventh of the contents per hour (follow the
  supplier)" — the same rule as kit MEMFAB0022B; some suppliers give
  one-fifth.
- Shades: gas work 3–5; plasma about 5 up to 40 A, 6 at 40–60 A, 8 at
  60–300 A (AWS shade-chart figures).
- Preheat measured "often about 75 mm" from the joint; steel expansion
  0.012 mm per metre per °C; flame-straightening limit for mild steel "dull red,
  about 600–650 °C".
- Kerf "often about 1.5–4 mm"; inner cones 2–3 mm off the plate (as the bank).
- Plasma: "on most machines one side of the kerf is squarer"; aluminium on a
  water table gives hydrogen (bank T2-S06).

## MEMFAB0062B Weld using GTAW (TIG)

- Tungsten colour bands follow ISO 6848 (green pure, red 2% thoriated, grey 2%
  ceriated, gold 1.5% and blue 2% lanthanated, white zirconiated); the kit
  warns that makers differ. P-17 relies on blue = 2% lanthanated.
- Tungsten DCEN ranges (overlapping, typical): 1.0 mm up to about 60 A,
  1.6 mm about 40–130 A, 2.4 mm about 80–220 A, 3.2 mm about 150–300 A.
- Inverter AC balance "often about 65–75% EN"; AC frequency "often about
  60–200 Hz"; pulse background "about 25–50% of peak".
- Post-flow "about 1 second per 10 A"; torch flow "often about 7–12 L/min";
  water-cooled torches "often over about 150–200 A"; TIG shade "often about
  10–12".
- Purge: oxygen limit taken from the WPS (case uses 200 ppm); purge kept on
  "for the root and at least the next run".
- Heat-tint colour meanings are general; the acceptable colour depends on the
  job's standard.
- Pickling pastes described as containing nitric and hydrofluoric acids; the
  kit sends trainees to the SDS and the Centre's rules.

## MEMFAB0063B Perform welding/fabrication inspection

- All acceptance limits in the kit are stated "for this job" (porosity totals,
  undercut 0.5 mm, misalignment 10%, cap height 3 mm, bend-test opening 3 mm).
- PT temperature range "often about 10–50 °C"; AC yoke lift test "often about
  4.5 kg"; visual inspection with the eye "within about 600 mm".
- Bolt grades given as metric property classes (8.8, 4.6). Check which bolt
  standards the Centre's drawings use (metric or ASTM).
- Part-turn example "for example a half turn" — the actual turn depends on
  bolt length and the specification.
- Steel expansion example: a 10 m beam 20 °C hotter is about 2.4 mm longer.

## MEMFAB0071B Undertake fabrication, forming, bending and shaping (basic)

- Air-bending rules of thumb: V-die about 8 × thickness; inside radius roughly
  one-sixth of the V opening; minimum flange "often roughly 0.7 × the V
  opening"; stainless "about half as much again" force as mild steel. Check
  against the Centre's press brake tooling chart.
- K-factor "often about 0.33–0.5"; relief cut "a little deeper than the bend
  radius plus the thickness".
- Blue heat "roughly 200–400 °C"; colour table: dull red about 600–700 °C,
  bright to light cherry red about 850–950 °C, burnt well over 1 200 °C.
- Cold pipe bending without a mandrel "to about 3–5 × the outside diameter"
  (centre-line radius) — follow the bender maker's chart.
- Notch for an angle corner = 180° − corner angle (P-13: 120° for a 60° corner).
- All sums were worked twice: brackets 95.1 mm, shell 1 869.5 mm, ring
  1 552 mm, 45° arc at 160 mm ≈ 125.6 mm, stainless bend 49.5 t, sheet 90.4 kg.
