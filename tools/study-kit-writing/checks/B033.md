# Batch B033: what an instructor should check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 3 kits, all
`"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by maker or job are written as
typical, with "follow the maker's instructions and your instructor". The
points below are the ones the writer was least sure of, plus anything in the
exam banks that looks wrong.

## MEMCOR0091A Draw and interpret sketches and simple drawings

- Terminal numbering is the usual IEC scheme: A1–A2 coil; 13–14 first NO
  auxiliary; 21–22 first NC; 95–96 overload NC; 97–98 overload NO. The kit
  says North American devices may be marked differently.
- AWG to mm²: 14 AWG 2.1, 12 AWG 3.3, 10 AWG 5.3, 8 AWG 8.4, 6 AWG 13.3 mm²
  (rounded). Imperial scales: 1/4" = 1'-0" given as 1:48, 1/8" as 1:96.
- Smoke detectors "about 1 m" from AC supply grilles: a common rule of thumb,
  not a code figure. Check against the fire-alarm practice the Centre teaches.
- IP table: first digit 7 and 8 do not exist for solids (stated); NEMA 3R
  and 4X described in one line each.
- Pilot lamp colours: the kit says they vary (red for 'running' in some North
  American plants; green normal under IEC) and defers to the legend.
- Jamaica: GER and licensed inspector before JPS connection; drawings from
  both North American and IEC practice, as the bank's T2-E08 assumes.
- Exam bank: T1-17 (7.2 m in about 300 mm) accepts 1:25 (288 mm); 1:20 would
  be 360 mm, so the key is right. No errors found.

## MEMCOR0093A Plan and organize work

- Unit title keeps the bank's spelling "organize"; the notes use "organise".
- Working hours "about 7:00 or 7:30 a.m. to 4:00 or 4:30 p.m."; rainy seasons
  "roughly May to June and September to November"; hurricane season
  1 June to 30 November; emergency numbers 110 (fire/ambulance) and 119.
- Contingency "about 10% is common for straightforward work".
- First-fix hold point, then GER inspection, then JPS connection, in the
  sequence flow: confirm the order the Centre teaches.
- Heat-stress advice (stop, shade, cool, water; call 110 if confused or
  collapsed) is general first-aid guidance.
- Worked sums: P-04 (25 + 140 + 40 min from 2:45 p.m. = 6:10 p.m.), P-08
  (30 × 45 min × 2 people = 45 labour-hours), P-10 (236 × 1.1 = 259.6 m,
  3 drums), P-14 (11/32 ≈ 34%), P-17 (96 ÷ 3.5 ≈ 27). Checked twice.
- Exam bank: T1-24 says 7:30 start + 30 min + 5 h work + 1 h lunch = 2:00 p.m.,
  which is correct. No errors found.

## MEMCOR0101A Prepare basic engineering drawing

- Tapping and clearance drills: M5 4.2/5.5; M6 5.0/6.6; M8 6.8/9; M10 8.5/11;
  M12 10.2/13.5 (ISO coarse, "normal" clearance). The bank's T1-28 uses
  11 mm for M10, which agrees.
- Hexagon across corners ≈ 1.155 × across flats.
- Bend allowance: worked example uses a shop-supplied deduction of 3.6 mm
  for 2 mm steel at 2 mm inside radius, and a rough formula
  1.57 × (r + 0.4 t) for the bend length (k-factor 0.4) as a fallback; both
  give 76.4 mm. Case c2 uses a stated deduction of 2 mm for 1.2 mm sheet.
  These are illustrative; the maker's chart governs.
- Projection symbol: first angle has the two circles to the right of the
  cone's side view, third angle to the left (as described). Check against
  the Centre's standard before it is taught.
- Cabinet oblique depth at half length, 45°; isometric axes at 30°.
- Paper sizes A0–A4 to the nearest mm.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0103A Maintain quality systems within a team

- ISO 9001 is named as the best-known quality-system standard, without
  clause numbers or certification detail.
- Calibration interval "often 12 months; the firm's procedure says".
- Insulation-tester example (reading 20% or 30% high; 1.1 MΩ could really
  be 0.9 MΩ) is illustrative; the kit says the procedure decides what is
  retested. Record retention "often several years"; the worked example uses
  6 years (bank T2-S08 uses 5).
- Chase-depth and torque advice is general; the torque figure always comes
  from the device maker.
- Worked percentages: 84 000/1 200 000 = 7%; 66/75 = 88%; 3/60 = 5%;
  24→18 = 25% fall; 27/36 = 75%; Pareto 20/26 ≈ 77%; 9/60 = 15%, 1/50 = 2%.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMCOR0111A Use power tools

- Jamaican 110 V is the full mains, not a reduced centre-tapped site supply;
  the kit says so and asks for GFCI/RCD protection.
- 50/60 Hz: brush-motor hand tools "usually run well on 50 Hz"; induction
  motors run about one-sixth slower and may overheat. Confirm with the
  Centre's practice on imported 60 Hz machines.
- Current examples use I = P ÷ V on 110 V (1 100 W = 10 A; 2 500 W ≈ 23 A).
- Vibration: EU/UK action value 2.5 m/s² and limit 5 m/s² (8-hour A(8)) are
  used "as a guide where there is no local figure"; exposure times from
  8 h × (2.5 ÷ a)². Noise "typically 95 to 105 dB(A)".
- Respirator minimum "N95 or P2"; no seal over a beard.
- Chase depth rule (one-third of wall thickness vertical, one-sixth
  horizontal) is a common masonry rule of thumb, not a Jamaican code figure.
- Fire watch after hot work "often 30 to 60 minutes".
- SDS-plus up to "about 26–30 mm" bits; disc speed rating must be at least
  the grinder's.
- Post-tensioned slabs: never drilled without the engineer's written approval.
- Exam bank: T1-11 says a 1 500 W, 110 V tool draws "about 14 A" (13.6 A),
  correct. No errors found.
