# Batch B037: what an instructor should check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 3 kits, all
`"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by maker, code or job are given as
typical, with "follow the specification, the maker's data and your instructor".
Every sum in the notes and questions was worked twice.

## MEMINS0162A Cut fit and install trunking system

- Fill limits: 45% (British practice) and 20% (NEC wireway) are both taught,
  with "the specification decides". The NEC grouping point (more than 30
  current-carrying conductors) is stated briefly; confirm it suits the Centre.
- Cable and trunking factors in the tables (12, 16, 22; 570, 1,037, 1,555, 2,091)
  are example figures, like the exam bank's.
- PVC trunking upper temperature "about 60 °C" and expansion figures (PVC about
  0.07 mm per m per °C, steel about 0.012) are typical; the maker's data wins.
- Site-made steel bends: the method is taught as in the exam bank (V cut in the
  side walls, fold the base, backing plate). Trade names for flat, internal and
  external bends vary, and the kit says to follow the Centre's method.
- Fire barrier inside the trunking plus fire stopping outside at fire-rated walls
  is taught as good practice; the product is whatever the specification approves.
- Data cable minimum bend radius "often about four times the diameter" is a
  general figure; use the cable data sheet.
- Exam bank: T1-45 asks where the continuity result is recorded and is fine.
  T1-14 (14.2 m plus about a tenth in 3 m lengths = 6) is correct (15.6 ÷ 3 = 5.2,
  round up). No errors found.

## MEMINS0172A Prepare and install cable trays - basic

- Load tables, spans and expansion coefficients (steel 0.012, aluminium
  0.023 mm per m per °C) are example or typical figures; the maker's tables rule.
- Splice position "within about a quarter of the span from a support" is a
  common maker rule, not universal.
- Tray bonding wording follows the exam bank ("bonded, not used as a protective
  conductor"); confirm against the Centre's specification practice.
- Fibreglass tray cannot be bonded (non-conductive): confirm no earth path is
  wanted in the Centre's examples.
- Exam bank: T1-22 (six 25 mm and eight 15 mm cables, answer 270 mm) is correct
  for touching cables in one layer (150 + 120). T2-E03 rounds 7.5 spans to 8 and
  gives 9 supports, which is correct. No errors found.

## MEMINS0262A Install distribution panels, metering sockets, terminal mains and meter earthing systems

- Jamaica claims: JPS owns the service and meter and only JPS may connect,
  disconnect or remove it; the customer's electrician installs the socket,
  terminal mains and panels; licensed-inspector sign-off under the GER before
  connection (same wording as the pilot EEMELS0011B kit). Check against JPS's
  current service requirements.
- Earth electrode: 25 Ω single-rod limit (NEC style), 2.4 m rod, test spikes
  "often 25 to 30 m" and 62% rule for the potential spike. Confirm the Centre's
  test method and acceptance value.
- Insulation test 500 V DC with "about 1 megohm" as a common pass value; the NEC
  sets no field figure, so confirm the Centre's value.
- Sub-panel neutral and earth separation and the main-bond rules are taught as
  in the exam bank and the NEC; the exact bonding arrangement at the main panel
  (where the earthing conductor lands) varies, so the kit keeps it general.
- Panel working space "about a metre deep" is a typical figure from the NEC.
- Exam bank: T1-12 uses 2.5 mV per amp per metre without a "per metre of
  circuit" note (the kit says the figure already counts both conductors). T1-02
  (34 ways + 20% = 40.8, so 42-way) is correct. T1-41 (neutral 35 − 25 = 10 A)
  is correct. No errors found.

## MEMMAH0042A Order materials

- Waste allowance "about 5 to 10%" for cable and lead-time examples are
  illustrative. Cable coil sizes (100 m), conduit bundles of 10 and 500 ft rolls
  follow the exam bank and Jamaican supply practice; metres to feet uses 3.28.
- Case prices (J$215 per metre, J$210 per metre) are made up for sums.
- Approval limits, quarantine and returns-authorisation steps describe common
  practice, not a legal requirement; the Centre's own procedure may differ.
- Imported items: shipping, customs and duty are named but no rates are given.
- Stock-level example uses the exam bank's formula (reorder level = use in lead
  time + safety stock). Maximum minus stock left at arrival gives the order
  quantity; some stores order a fixed quantity instead.
- Exam bank: T2-S03 (40 m + 10% = 44 m, 14.7, so 15 lengths) is correct. T1-11
  (6 weeks before 30 June, with a week spare = 12 May) is correct. No errors found.

## MEMMAH0071A Perform manual handling and lifting

- One-person lifting guide figures (about 25 kg for men, 16 kg for women) and
  the team guide (two people about two-thirds, three about a half of the sum of
  their capacities) are common guidance, not a Jamaican standard; confirm with
  the Centre's rules.
- Sling load at a 60° included angle (leg load about 1.15 × half the load) is
  correct; the kit tells trainees to use the tag and maker's table.
- The kit says employers must protect workers and workers must report hazards,
  without naming a Jamaican Act. Check whether the Centre wants the current law
  named.
- Emergency numbers 110 (ambulance and fire) and 119 (police), as in the pilot
  EEMELS0011B kit.
- Exam bank: T1-10 (150 kg ÷ 18 kg = 8.3, so 8) is correct. T1-11 (10 × 3 m at
  0.9 kg per metre = 27 kg) is correct. No errors found.
