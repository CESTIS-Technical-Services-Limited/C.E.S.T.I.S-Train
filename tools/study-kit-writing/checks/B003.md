# Batch B003: what an instructor should check

All five kits are `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by maker or job are
written as typical, with "follow the maker and your instructor". The points
below are the ones the writer was least sure of, plus problems in the exam
banks.

## MEMCOR0171D Use and maintain graduated measuring devices

- This kit is shared: `copy-shared.js` will file it under
  electrical-installation too. The text names no qualification, and the
  examples come from both trades. The practice questions were also checked
  against the electrical bank (no overlap).
- Electrical figures: 12 AWG solid copper about 2.05 mm, 14 AWG about
  1.63 mm, 2.5 mm² about 1.78 mm; ¾-inch PVC conduit about 26.7 mm outside
  (Schedule 40 size). Check these match the conduit and cable used locally.
- Steel expansion "about 0.012 mm per metre per °C"; reference temperature
  20 °C; digital calipers "accurate to about 0.02–0.03 mm".
- Calibration "often once a year". The kit says the Bureau of Standards
  Jamaica offers calibration services: confirm.
- Jamaican claims: feet and inches still common in hardware stores, steel in
  20-foot (about 6 m) lengths, 4 ft × 8 ft sheets.
- Bevel reading: the kit says a 30° weld bevel "shows 60°" when measured
  from the plate face. Check this wording against how the Centre teaches it.
- Exam bank: no errors found. T1-40 keys the vernier caliper as "most exact"
  for a 20 mm bar only because no micrometer is offered.

## MEMDDD0042C Prepare basic mechanical drawings

- Projection symbols are described as: first angle, trapezium on the left and
  circles on the right; third angle, circles on the left. Check against the
  Centre's textbook.
- Typical figures: line weights 0.7/0.35 or 0.5/0.25 mm; lettering 3.5 mm
  (titles 5–7 mm); projection-line gap about 1 mm and overrun 2–3 mm;
  dimension lines about 10 mm from the outline then 8 mm apart; arrowheads
  about 3 mm; hatching 3–4 mm apart; border about 10 mm.
- Welding symbols are taught to the AWS rule (symbol below the line = arrow
  side). ISO 2553 drawings differ. Confirm which one the Centre uses.
- The standards are named as ISO / BS 8888 / ASME. Confirm which one the
  Centre follows.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMFAB0022C Perform advanced manual thermal cutting, gouging and shaping

- Filter shades (from the American guide figures): oxy-fuel cutting 3–4 up to
  25 mm, 4–5 for 25–150 mm, 5–6 over 150 mm; plasma 4–6 up to 60 A and 8–9
  for 60–300 A; arc gouging about 12 (at least 10).
- Kindling temperature about 870–900 °C. Flame temperatures: acetylene about
  3 100 °C, propane about 2 800 °C. Acetylene never above about 1 bar
  (15 psi).
- Shut-down order is given as "many makers say fuel gas first, then oxygen",
  and for a sustained backfire "oxygen first". Practice varies, so check it
  against the Centre's torches and procedure. Hose colours: oxygen blue
  (green on American outfits), fuel red.
- Arc gouging: air about 5.5–7 bar; current about 150–400 A for 4–6 mm
  electrodes; push angle 35–45°; groove about 3 mm wider than the electrode.
  Stick-out is left as "within the maker's limits".
- Plasma: stand-off about 1.5–3 mm. The squarer edge is on the right of
  travel, but this depends on the torch.
- Kerf: oxy-fuel about 1.5–3 mm, plasma about 1–2.5 mm. Fire watch: at
  least 30 minutes, the same as the pilot kit.
- Jamaican claim: LPG and propane are "the same family" as local cooking gas.
- Exam bank: T2-S15 accepts "cooking gas" and "bottled gas" as answers for
  propane. That marking may be too loose.

## MEMFAB0041C Carry out mechanical cutting operations – (basic)

- On a guillotine, stainless steel is given as "often only about two-thirds"
  of the mild-steel capacity.
- Aviation snips: the colour code (yellow straight, red left, green right) is
  given as "often". Capacity is "often about 1.2 mm mild steel".
- Kerf allowances: hacksaw about 1 mm, power saws and discs 1–3 mm. Hacksaw
  stroke rate is about one a second.
- Abrasive discs: a disc that has been soaked is not used; check use-by dates;
  a new disc runs "for a short time" first, per the Centre's rule. The
  bandsaw chip-colour guide is general shop guidance.
- The kit does not say which way power-hacksaw teeth point. It says to follow
  the machine's marking.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMFAB0042C Weld in the flat and horizontal positions (MMAW)

- Filter shade: this bank (T1-01, T2-S10) keys **shade 10** for a 3.2 mm
  electrode at 100–125 A, which is the American guide figure. The pilot kit
  MEMFAB0111C gives **11** for 80–175 A, from the European chart. This kit
  teaches 10 and says some charts give 11. Make the pilot and this kit agree.
- Single-V preparation follows the bank: root face 2–3 mm and gap 2.5–3 mm.
  The pilot kit has root face 1–2 mm and gap 2–3 mm.
- E7018: out of the oven no more than about 4 hours; holding oven
  120–150 °C; re-bake about 350 °C for 1–2 hours (the same as the pilot).
  Typical E7018 currents: 2.5 mm 70–100 A, 3.2 mm 100–140 A,
  4.0 mm 140–190 A.
- Single-run fillet limits are about 8 mm leg in 2F and about 10 mm in 1F.
  Penetration bead about 1–2 mm; cap reinforcement no more than about 3 mm;
  pre-set "a few degrees".
- 2G electrode angles are described only in words (lower beads point
  slightly up, top bead near level or slightly down). The tip that the root
  gap closes up ahead of the weld is left to the instructor.
- Exam bank: no errors found, apart from the shade conflict with the pilot.
