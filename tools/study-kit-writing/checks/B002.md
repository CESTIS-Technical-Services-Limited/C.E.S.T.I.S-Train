# B002: what an instructor should double-check

All five kits are `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by maker, standard or job are written as typical, with "follow the drawing / the supplier / your instructor". The points below are the ones least certain. No clear errors were found in the five exam banks; their worked figures (diagonals, circumferences, hole positions, dates) were checked and are right.

## MEMCOR0092C Mark off/out structural fabrication and shapes

- Dot (prick) punch point "often about 60°", centre punch "about 90°".
- Kerf: hacksaw or band saw about 1–2 mm, abrasive disc about 1–3 mm, plasma and oxy-fuel "more"; the kit says to check on a test cut.
- A 6 m steel bar warming by 30 °C grows about 2 mm (12 × 10⁻⁶ per °C).
- Jamaica: steel usually sold in 6 m (20 ft) lengths; suppliers and drawings may use inches and gauges; US shapes such as W8 × 15 and C6 × 8.2 are met.
- Section examples: UB 203 × 133 × 25, UC 152 × 152 × 23, 150 × 75 PFC (18 kg/m), 60.3 × 3.9 CHS = 2 in schedule 40. Case c4 uses a 45 mm back mark on a 75 mm angle leg.
- General fabrication tolerance "about ±1 mm or ±2 mm"; bolt holes "usually a little bigger than the bolt".
- Pipe mitre check: for a 45° cut the curve's height difference equals the outside diameter. Cone frustum example (Ø600 base, Ø200 top, 400 high): slant 670.8 mm and 223.6 mm, pattern angle about 161°.

## MEMCOR0101C Prepare basic engineering drawing

- Taught to British/ISO practice: thick and thin lines about 0.7/0.35 mm or 0.5/0.25 mm; lettering about 3.5 mm (titles 5–7 mm); border about 10 mm; projection lines start about 1 mm from the outline and run 2–3 mm past; arrowheads about 3 mm; dimension lines 8–10 mm apart; hatching about 3–5 mm apart.
- Projection symbols: in first angle the two circles sit beside the large end of the trapezium, in third angle beside the small end. Check against the Centre's own title block.
- Claim that most Caribbean training centres teach first angle and US drawings use third angle.
- Weld symbols are mentioned only briefly (ISO and AWS differ); the instructor shows the Centre's system.
- The bank (T1-24, T2-E04) expects projection lines to start with a gap, as in BS and ASME practice; some ISO drawings run them to the outline.

## MEMCOR0121D Classify engineering materials (basic)

- Carbon ranges follow the bank: mild 0.1–0.3%, medium 0.3–0.6%, high 0.6% to over 1%, cast iron 2–4% (some books put mild steel at up to 0.25%).
- Densities and melting points in the table are rounded (for example cast iron 1 150–1 250 °C, bronze 950–1 050 °C).
- Stainless: at least about 10.5% chromium; grade 316 (with molybdenum) recommended near the sea.
- Tempering colours and the tools listed against them (pale straw 220–230 °C to blue 290–300 °C) differ between charts.
- Spark descriptions for stainless steel and high-speed steel; bench grinder tool rest "no more than about 3 mm".
- Simplified galvanic order: zinc, aluminium, steel, lead, brass, copper and bronze, stainless steel.
- Conversions: 18 ga ≈ 1.2 mm, 16 ga ≈ 1.5 mm, 14 ga ≈ 1.9 mm (steel sheet); sheet and plate split "often about 3 mm"; chequer plate thickness convention left to the supplier.
- Overhead lines: aluminium "about half the weight of copper for the same current".
- Jamaica: many "wrought iron" gates are really mild steel; lead paint possible on old railings.

## MEMCOR0122D Write basic technical reports (shared with Electrical Installation)

- Jamaica's data protection law is referred to by its purpose only.
- Dates: day/month/year in Jamaica; the kit advises writing the month in words for readers abroad.
- The insulation resistance example (200 MΩ) gives no pass or fail value on purpose.
- The sample incident uses burn cooling for 20 minutes and cling film, as in the OH&S kit.
- All weekdays given for 2026 dates were checked.

## MEMCOR0141D Follow principles of OH&S (shared with Electrical Installation)

- Law: the kit names the Factories Act and the Ministry of Labour and Social Security's Occupational Safety and Health Department, and says a wider OSH law "has been in preparation". Check its current status.
- Emergency numbers 110 (fire and ambulance) and 119 (police), as in the electrical pilot.
- RCD 30 mA; GFCI about 5 mA.
- Lifting guide figures of about 25 kg (men) and 16 kg (women), close to the body at waist height, are from UK guidance.
- Noise rule of thumb: having to shout to be heard at about 2 m.
- Ladders: 1 out for every 4 up; about 1 m above the landing; not the top three rungs of a leaning ladder or the top two steps of a stepladder.
- Chemical splash in the eye: rinse "for at least 15 minutes or as the SDS says" (sources give 10–20 minutes).
- Heat illness "especially from May to October"; heat stroke signs and first aid.
- Extinguishers: CO2 described as not suited to wood, paper and cloth; dry powder not for cooking fat; US and British class letters differ (C).
- Hurricane season 1 June to 30 November; dengue, chikungunya, Zika and leptospirosis hazards.
