# Batch B069: what an instructor should check

Five Business Administration (Management) Level 5 kits, all `"status": "to-check"`.
Laws and bodies are named only by purpose; no sections, penalties or tax rates
are quoted. The points below are the ones the writer was least sure of, plus
anything in the exam banks that looks wrong.

## PSAHRD0214B Manage performance

- Jamaican practice: the appraisal year "normally runs April to March" (with
  the financial year), and the public service has its own performance
  management and appraisal system with set forms, rating bands and dates. The
  kit stays general; check the current forms and rating bands the Centre uses.
- The staged poor-performance procedure (informal stage, formal meeting with
  invitation letter, improvement plan, written warning and final review,
  then redeployment, demotion or termination) follows the exam bank. Check it
  against the current public service rules and the code of discipline, and the
  right to be accompanied by a union or staff association delegate.
- Typical review periods are not given as figures ("weeks for simple tasks,
  months for complex work"); confirm what the policy says.
- Moderation versus forced distribution: the kit says many public bodies prefer
  moderation with evidence. Check against the body's actual practice.
- Exam bank: T1-24 says the review period should be "as policy sets", which
  gives no figure; the bank is correct but vague. All the bank's sums were
  checked and are right (3.6, 95%, 3.7, 4.3, 80%, 20%, 4.0).

## PSAHRD0224B Develop and use emotional intelligence

- Goleman's five-part model (self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation,
  empathy, social skills) and the term "amygdala hijack" are as the exam bank
  uses them. The 1990s dating is Goleman's best-known book; check if the
  Centre prefers another model (for example four-domain or Mayer-Salovey).
- Johari window (four panes), 360-degree feedback, emotional labour,
  psychological safety: standard definitions; no figures.
- Breathing pattern "in for four, out for six" is a typical example, not a
  prescribed figure.
- Support for stressed staff: the kit says to refer to a doctor or the
  employee assistance programme and gives no phone numbers. Check which
  services the Centre's trainees' employers actually have, and add a local
  helpline if the Centre wants one.
- Exam bank: SA "Feedback ... 360 degrees" and the average of 3, 4, 2, 4, 2
  (= 3.0) are correct. T1-32 treats an EI test score as "one view"; the kit
  agrees.

## PSSADM0144B Exercise delegations

- Terms "accounting officer" (Permanent Secretary or head of body for the
  money) and the Mayor and chief executive of a municipal corporation: confirm
  the current titles used in Jamaica (the post titled "Secretary-Manager"
  before 2016 may still be in use in some documents).
- The kit says the delegator "usually may still use the power himself, unless
  the law says otherwise" (matches the exam bank T1-24). Confirm this against
  the Centre's legal guidance, since some statutory powers cannot be shared.
- Statement that an act outside the delegation "may be invalid" and may be
  ratified by someone who holds the power: general principle only; ratification
  depends on the law and body.
- Emergency purchasing is described only as "the emergency procedure"; check
  the ministry's actual emergency rules.
- All sums were checked: J$850,000 room left; J$1,350,000 and J$1,800,000
  contract values; 12.5% overturned; 15% changed on review.
- Exam bank: none found wrong. T1-04 ("the Permanent Secretary, with the
  director" remain accountable) is acceptable but depends on the body.

## PSSADM0165B Manage innovation and continuous improvement

- Classification of innovation types and degrees (incremental, radical,
  disruptive), the diffusion-of-innovations groups, the eight wastes of Lean and
  the DMAIC phases are standard textbook content; check the version the Centre
  teaches (some lists use seven wastes).
- Statement that a certificate shows a quality system "has been audited as
  meeting" an international standard: kept general; no standard number named.
- Public-sector ownership of tools made at work (the agency normally owns
  them): general rule, depends on employment terms and the law.
- Jamaica: "public bodies are working to move more services online" is a
  general statement; no programme named.
- All sums were checked: weighted scores 7.55, 6.05, 5.65 (c1) and 6.55, 6.95,
  6.25 (notes); payback 12 and 13 months and 4 years; 30% and 40% reductions;
  16% implementation rate; J$600 per application.
- Exam bank: T1-18 asks which diffusion group a few keen officers who use a new
  system at once and help colleagues belong to, and gives "early adopters";
  "innovators" could also be argued.

## PSSADM0175A Develop partnering arrangements

- The Jamaica note says the Government has a public-private partnership policy
  and agencies that support such projects, with special approvals for large
  deals. Confirm the current policy and bodies before the Centre states more.
- Definitions of letter of intent, MoU (usually not binding for money
  obligations), SLA, PPP and joint venture are general. Whether an MoU is
  binding depends on its wording; check with the legal unit.
- Dispute resolution order (partnership managers, senior officers, mediation,
  then other remedies) follows the exam bank T1-44; check against the
  agreements the Centre uses.
- The continuum labels (networking, coordinating, cooperating, collaborating,
  integrating) vary between authors; the exam bank uses "networking or
  coordinating, at the looser end".
- All sums were checked: weighted scores 7.35, 7.85, 6.95; J$15,000,000,
  J$9,000,000 and J$6,000,000 shares; J$1,760,000 in-kind; J$4,240,000 cash;
  J$4,000,000 match; 70%, 64%, 80%; J$20,000, J$18,750 and J$8,000 per person.
- Exam bank: SA on "term" ("agreed length of time") and "match funding" are
  correct; T1-42 ("home addresses of staff") is correct as the item not
  usually included.
