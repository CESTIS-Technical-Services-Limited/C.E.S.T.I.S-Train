# Batch B018: what an instructor should check

Five Welding Level 3 kits, all `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by
maker, code or job are written as typical, with "follow the WPS, the maker's
instructions and your instructor". The points below are the ones the writer
was least sure of, plus anything in the exam banks that looks wrong.

## MEMFAB0072B Perform advanced welding using oxyacetylene welding process (OAW)

- Nozzle chart (British-style numbers): 2 → about 1.2 mm, 57 L/h; 3 → 2 mm,
  86 L/h; 5 → 2.5 mm, 140 L/h; 7 → 3 mm, 200 L/h; 10 → 4 mm, 280 L/h;
  13 → 5 mm, 370 L/h; 18 → 6.5 mm, 520 L/h. Also the claim that the number is
  roughly cubic feet per hour. Check against the Centre's own torch chart.
- Acetylene withdrawal "often one-seventh of the contents per hour" for
  continuous work (some suppliers use another fraction). Cylinder sizes in the
  cases (7 000 L acetylene, 6 800 L oxygen) are examples.
- Acetylene never above about 1 bar (15 psi); flammable range "about 2.5 % to
  over 80 %"; oxygen "often around 200 bar" when full.
- Open an acetylene cylinder valve "often no more than about one and a half
  turns". Arrestors "tested as the maker says (often yearly)".
- Normal shut-down "commonly acetylene first"; sustained backfire and
  flashback: torch oxygen valve first. This matches the Level 2 kit
  MEMFAB0072C. Confirm against the Centre's procedure.
- A hot acetylene cylinder: no exclusion distance is given; the kit leaves it
  to the fire service (110).
- Torch angles: leftward 60–70°, rightward 40–50°, rod 30–40°. Leftward up to
  about 5 mm. Leftward V "often about 80°", rightward about 60°.
- Braze welding: rod melts at about 870–900 °C; not for parts that work above
  "roughly 250 °C". Grey cast iron "about 2–4 %" carbon; fusion-weld preheat
  "often a dull red". Aluminium melts at about 660 °C, its oxide above 2 000 °C.
- Exam bank: T2-S04 accepts "cracking" the valve for both gases. Many
  suppliers say not to crack an acetylene valve, or to do it only outdoors away
  from flames. The kit says to follow the supplier.

## MEMFAB0081B Assemble fabricated components (basic)

- Bolt table: 4.6 = 400/240 MPa, 8.8 = 800/640 MPa, 10.9 = 1 000/900 MPa.
  Matching nut classes are given as 4 or 5, 8 and 10. For 8.8 bolts above M16,
  the standard tensile figure is 830 MPa; the kit gives "at least 800".
- Clearance holes "often 1–2 mm" bigger than the bolt. "At least one full
  thread" past the nut.
- Rivets: edge distance "at least 2 diameters" and pitch "often at least
  3 diameters" are given as common rules.
- Steel growth 0.012 mm per metre per °C. Steel 7 850 kg/m³ (7.85 kg per m²
  per mm). Galvanising bath "about 450 °C".
- Weld symbols follow AWS (below the line = arrow side). The kit notes that
  ISO drawings use a dashed line instead.
- Manual handling: no weight limit is given; check against the Centre's rules.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMFAB0102B Perform MMAW to AWS specification (low carbon steel sheet)

- The overview names the AWS sheet steel code (D1.3) once, with its scope "up
  to about 4.8 mm (3/16 in)" and the plate code "from about 3 mm". The brief
  asks for "the AWS code the Centre uses". Remove the name if it is not wanted.
- Gauge table (plain steel sheet): 10 = 3.4, 12 = 2.7, 14 = 1.9, 16 = 1.5,
  18 = 1.2, 20 = 0.9, 22 = 0.8, 24 = 0.6 mm. Galvanised gauges differ slightly.
- Electrode and current table for sheet: 1.0–1.5 mm sheet → 1.6–2.0 mm
  electrode, about 25–55 A; 1.5–2.5 mm sheet → 2.0–2.5 mm, about 40–75 A;
  2.5–4 mm sheet → 2.5–3.2 mm, about 60–110 A (E6013).
- Low carbon steel "about 0.25 % carbon or less". Tack spacing "about 25–50 mm"
  on thin sheet. Fire watch "often at least 30–60 minutes".
- Weld washers "on very thin deck": the code's thickness limit (about 0.7 mm)
  is not stated. A qualification "commonly" lapses after six months without
  using the process. A flat test "generally" does not cover vertical work.
- Copper picked up from a backing bar is said to cause cracking.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMFAB0103B Perform MMAW to weld to AWS specification (alloy steel plate)

- CE guide: "up to about 0.40 seldom needs preheat; above about 0.45 usually
  does". Worked CE examples (0.48 and, in case c3, 0.47) were each worked twice.
- The preheat table in case c3 is made up for the exercise and labelled as the
  WPS table.
- Electrode suffixes: A1 about 0.5 % Mo; B2 about 1.25 % Cr and 0.5 % Mo;
  C1 about 2.5 % Ni; C2 about 3.5 % Ni; C3 about 1 % Ni; W for weathering steel;
  M for military-type high-strength grades. E8018 = about 550 MPa.
- Time out of the oven: "about 4 hours for E70XX and 2 hours for E80XX" under
  the AWS structural code, unless tested for longer. Holding oven "often at
  least about 120 °C".
- HAZ hardness limit "around 350 HV is common in structural work". Waiting time
  before the final crack check is "often a day or two".
- Single-V on backing "often 45° to 60° included".
- Positions covered by plate groove tests: 1G F; 2G F and H; 3G F, H and V;
  4G F and OH; 3G and 4G together cover all positions.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMFAB0111B Perform basic welding using MMAW (Level 3)

- Duty cycle at another current ≈ rated duty × (rated current ÷ new
  current)², capped at 100 %. Example: 200 A at 40 % gives about 62 % at
  160 A. Cable sizes are left to the maker's table.
- OCV "about 50–90 V", arc voltage "about 20–30 V". Vertical current "often
  about 10–15 % less" than flat.
- Single-V: about 60° included, root face 1.5–2 mm, root gap 2.5–3 mm.
  Reinforcement "more than about 3 mm" is called excessive.
- E7024 is AC or DC and runs only flat and in horizontal fillets. E7018 is
  "not made for" downhill welding.
- Case c4 assumes the kitchen stainless steel is the non-magnetic (austenitic)
  kind, so dye penetrant is used instead of magnetic particle testing.
- Exam bank: T1 "In the code E7018, what does the last digit, 8, tell you?" The
  coating and current are given by the last two digits (18) together. The key
  ("the flux type and the current it can use") is acceptable, but the stem
  could say "the last two digits".
