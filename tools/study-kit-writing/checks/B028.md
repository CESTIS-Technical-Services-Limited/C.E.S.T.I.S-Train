# Batch B028: what an instructor should check

Five Electrical Installation and Maintenance Level 2 kits, all
`"status": "to-check"`. Code figures are quoted from the NEC because the
Jamaican code is based on it; the kits say "follow the drawing, the code and
the maker". The points below are the ones the writer was least sure of, plus
anything in the exam banks that looks wrong.

## MEMCOR0161D Plan to undertake a routine task

- Jamaica: "new installations, and larger alterations, are inspected and
  certified before JPS will connect or reconnect them", overseen by the GER.
  Check the wording on alterations matches current GER practice.
- Rainy seasons "roughly May to June and September to November"; hurricane
  season June to November; forecasts from the Meteorological Service of Jamaica.
- Time allowance for problems "10 to 15 per cent" and material allowance
  "about 10%" are rules of thumb, not standards.
- Simple 3 × 3 risk matrix (low / medium / high): check it matches the
  Centre's own risk assessment form.
- Symbols: S / S3 (US style) and British-style symbols are described in words
  only; the kit tells trainees the legend decides.
- Worked sums checked twice: 1:50 route 210 mm = 10.5 m, + 10% = 11.55 m,
  4 lengths of 3 m; 34 saddles = 2 packs of 25; plan 280 min = 4 h 40 min;
  3 battens in 90 min = 2 h over; 1:20 × 65 mm = 1.3 m.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMFAB0011D Perform manual soldering/de-soldering of electrical/electronic components

- Tip temperatures "about 300 to 350 °C" tin-lead and "about 340 to 380 °C"
  lead-free, as starting points.
- Melting points: 63/37 183 °C; 60/40 183 to 190 °C; SAC305 about 217 to
  220 °C; tin-copper about 227 °C.
- ESD: "you will not feel a discharge below about 3,000 volts"; wrist-strap
  resistor "usually about 1 megohm".
- Joint time "2 to 4 seconds"; bend leads "about 2 mm" from the body; trim
  leads to "about 1 mm" above the fillet. Check against the Centre's standard.
- Tantalum capacitors said to be marked on the positive side.
- Resistor table: violet, grey and white multipliers shown as "rarely used".
- Tip on adding fresh tin-lead solder to old (including lead-free) joints is
  qualified "where the Centre allows it".
- Statement that solder smoke is mostly flux and the main lead route is
  hand to mouth.
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMINS0011E Install, terminate and connect electrical wiring systems

- NEC figures used: hole edge at least 32 mm from a timber edge; NM cable
  fixed within 300 mm of boxes and every 1.4 m; NM bend radius 5 × diameter;
  about 6 mm of sheath inside a non-metallic box; 40% conduit fill; 150 mm
  free conductor at boxes; 14 / 12 / 10 AWG copper at 15 / 20 / 30 A; about
  3% voltage drop on a branch circuit.
- AWG to mm² equivalents (2.1, 3.3, 5.3, 8.4 mm²) are approximate.
- Voltage-drop example uses 12 AWG copper at about 5.2 ohms per 1,000 m.
- Jamaica: outlet circuits described as radial (no ring circuits). Check this
  matches local practice and older installations trainees may meet.
- Main bonding jumper wording: neutral and ground joined only at the service
  panel, separated in sub-panels.
- Insulation resistance: 500 V DC test, 1 MΩ quoted as "a common minimum"
  (a British/IEC figure; the NEC sets no value).
- GFCI LINE/LOAD statement that many types will not reset if miswired.
- Water heater example (3,000 W, 220 V, 20 A two-pole, 12 AWG) chosen so the
  NEC 125% rule for water heaters is met (17 A).
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMINS0051D Cut, bend and install electrical conduits

- Bender take-up "about 125 mm (5 in) for ½-inch EMT, 150 mm for ¾ inch,
  200 mm for 1 inch": typical of common hand benders; check the Centre's.
- Offset multipliers 5.8 / 2.6 / 2.0 / 1.4 / 1.2 and shrink 0.09 / 0.2 /
  0.27 / 0.41 / 0.58 (calculated; charts round them differently).
- Three-bend saddle rule: outer marks 2.5 × height, shrink about 0.19 ×
  height. Check against the Centre's bender chart.
- PVC expansion 0.06 mm per m per °C; expansion fitting at 6 mm or more (NEC).
  Saddles "snug but free to slide" with an expansion coupling: check against
  the maker's instructions.
- Support spacing (NEC): PVC ½ to 1 inch every 900 mm; EMT and steel within
  900 mm of boxes and every 3 m. The 360° bending limit is NEC; "British
  practice puts a draw-in point after two 90° bends" is general guidance.
- Blade choice: 18 or 24 TPI heavy-gauge, 24 or 32 TPI EMT.
- "In Jamaica's warm weather 20 mm and 25 mm PVC usually bends cold" (as in
  the exam bank).
- Exam bank: no errors found.

## MEMINS0062D Terminate and connect specialist cables

- Bending radius "about 6 times the overall diameter" for armoured and MI
  cable (XLPE armoured cable may need more); "about 4 times" for UTP.
- Data: Cat 6 10 Gb/s "about 55 m"; untwist no more than about 13 mm;
  pulling force about 110 N; 90 m + 10 m channel.
- F connector centre conductor "often about 2 mm" proud.
- Pre-insulated crimp colours red 0.5 to 1.5, blue 1.5 to 2.5, yellow 4 to
  6 mm²; ferrule colours said to vary by maker.
- MI cable: re-test "after about 24 hours"; driving out moisture with heat
  mentioned only for trained people with the instructor's permission.
- Fire alarm cables: red sheath "commonly" used; metal or fire-rated fixings.
  Check against the specifications the Centre uses in Jamaica.
- Coax screen earthed at the building entry "as the code usually requires".
- Screen earthed "usually at the control-panel end".
- Exam bank: no errors found.
