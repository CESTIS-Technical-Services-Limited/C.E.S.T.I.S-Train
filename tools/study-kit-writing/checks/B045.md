# Batch B045: what an instructor should check

Five Cosmetology Level 2 kits, all `"status": "to-check"`. Figures that vary by
maker or job are written as typical, with "follow the maker's instructions and
your instructor". CSBBAR0042D and CSBBTH0002D are also taught in Beauty
Therapy; their kits are meant to be copied to that programme.

## BSSCRE0322C Contribute to effective workplace relationships

- Jamaican law: the kit says employment law gives rights on pay (at least
  the national minimum wage), hours and leave, and that a law protects people
  from sexual harassment at work with employers expected to have a policy.
  Check the name, status and date of the harassment law, and whether the
  Centre wants it named.
- The Ministry of Labour and Social Security is described as advising workers
  and helping settle disputes. Check how the Centre refers clients to it.
- Religious and food examples (Sabbath from Friday sunset to Saturday sunset;
  Rastafari ital diet; no pork for Adventists and Muslims) are general
  statements and may not fit every person.
- Busy salon periods (Christmas, Easter, graduation, late-August back-to-school)
  are the writer's estimate.
- The kit teaches passive, aggressive and assertive styles, "I" statements and
  a ten-step problem-solving chain: these come from general workplace
  practice, not the unit standard.
- Exam bank: the win-win short answer asks how many of the two sides gain; "both"
  is right but the stem is awkward. The bias short answer accepts "prejudice";
  the kit treats them as related, not identical.

## CSBBAR0022D Perform hair shaping on excessively curly hair

- Guard sizes (#0.5 about 1.5 mm, #1 3 mm, #1.5 4.5 mm, #2 6 mm, #3 10 mm,
  #4 13 mm, #5 to #8 16 to 25 mm) and the lever "about half a guard step" are
  typical figures; makers differ. The exam bank also says a higher number
  leaves longer hair.
- Shrinkage example (a coil stretching to 8 or 9 cm and springing back to 3 or
  4 cm) is illustrative. The exam item uses 5 cm and 2 cm.
- Razor bumps, nape bumps ("barber's bumps"), keloids: described as not
  contagious, with a referral to a doctor when infected or growing. The kit
  does not name a nape condition; check the Centre's wording.
- Twist sponge: the kit says it cannot be disinfected properly between clients
  and follows "the Centre's rule". The exam bank lists the sponge as ordinary
  equipment; confirm the Centre's rule.
- Advice to return "in about two to three weeks" for a line-up or fade.
- Styptic: single-use powder or liquid only, never a shared pencil (matches the
  exam bank).
- Jamaica: school hair rules (parts, designs) vary by school; 110 V / 220 V,
  50 Hz.
- Exam bank: the guard questions assume a higher number leaves longer hair,
  which is right for most clippers but not for every make; the kit tells
  trainees to check the maker's chart.

## CSBBAR0042D Perform face shave

- Razor holds: the kit describes freehand (stroke towards you), backhand (palm
  up, stroke away) and reverse freehand (stroke upwards) in general terms and
  refers to the instructor. Check these against how the Centre teaches them.
- Blade angle "about 30 degrees" (from the exam bank); second time over
  "across the grain" only on calm skin (the exam bank says second pass, not
  its direction). Chin and upper lip shaved last because the hair is coarsest.
- Sharps container: closed at the fill line (usually about three-quarters
  full) and collected by an approved waste service. Check the Centre's and
  Jamaica's rules for clinical waste.
- Hepatitis B vaccination for barbers is suggested as advice; check the
  Centre's policy.
- Hot towel: tested on the inner wrist; "a minute or two" on the face is
  typical, not a fixed time.
- Contraindication lists (impetigo, tinea barbae, cold sores, infected bumps,
  sunburn, broken eczema) follow the exam bank plus sunburn and boils.
- Exam bank: the headrest-cover short answer accepts "towel" and "paper towel".
  The exam has no item on the fixed-blade (cut-throat) razor, which the kit says
  many shops and centres no longer allow for client work. Confirm the Centre's
  rule.

## CSBBTH0001B Provide basic manicure and pedicure service

- Nail growth: fingernails "four to six months" base to tip, toenails "a year
  or more".
- Foam buffers, orange sticks and toe separators described as single-use; the
  kit says pumice is single-use "or the client's own, as the Centre's rules
  say". Check the Centre's rule on pumice and metal foot files.
- Remover: acetone is described as faster and harsher than non-acetone.
- Polish dry time "at least ten minutes, longer for toes" is a guide.
- Referral for athlete's foot, verrucae, fungal nails and ingrown toenails to a
  doctor, pharmacist or podiatrist; the kit says that corn and hard-skin work
  with a blade is a podiatrist's job in Jamaica. Check this claim.
- Exam bank: the short answer for the wrapped wooden stick accepts "hoof stick"
  (and "cuticle stick"), which are different tools from an orange stick. Check
  the accepted answers.

## CSBBTH0002D Provide manicure and pedicure service

- Nail growth "about 3 mm a month" for fingernails; toenails slower.
- File grit: "the higher the number, the finer the file"; coarse about 80 to
  100, natural nails about 180 to 240. Check the Centre's files.
- Cuticle remover time and washing off follow the maker; the kit gives no
  minutes.
- Tapotement is taught as not normally used in a nail massage; the exam bank
  asks only for its name.
- Foot spa: drain, scrub, flush the jets, disinfect for the contact time,
  rinse, log. Check the Centre's cleaning-log form.
- A hot, red, painful calf is flagged as a possible blood clot and a reason to
  see a doctor; check the wording the Centre prefers.
- Exam bank: the cut-skin question says to press with cotton, then apply
  antiseptic; the kit follows it, but the barber units use styptic for nicks: check
  which the Centre wants. Paronychia is described as bacterial, but it can also
  be fungal (especially with long-term wet work). The cuticle-pusher short
  answer accepts "hoof stick".
- Beau's lines: the kit says they usually follow illness and grow out; the
  exam item says the same.
