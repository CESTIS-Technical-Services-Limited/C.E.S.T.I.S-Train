# Batch B013: points for the instructor to check

Welding Level 3 (MEM30215). Figures that vary by maker, standard or job are written as typical, with "follow the maker, the WPS or the Centre's rules".

## MEMCOR0101B Prepare basic engineering drawing

- Line widths 0.7/0.35 mm and 0.5/0.25 mm; lettering about 3.5 mm (notes) and 5–7 mm (titles); hatching about 3–4 mm apart; projection lines start about 1 mm from the outline and run 2–3 mm past the dimension line; arrowheads about 3 mm.
- Projection: both methods taught. The symbol is described only as "the two differ in where the end view of the cone sits". Confirm the Centre's method (the bank's checklist says first angle or the Centre's choice).
- Weld symbols taught as AWS (below the line = arrow side), with a short ISO note (dashed line; "a" = throat, "z" = leg). Back-weld symbol described as a small half circle.
- The scale-fit sums (P-01 and the notes) assume about 390 × 250 mm of usable space on an A3 sheet.
- Hexagon across corners ≈ 1.155 × across flats (P-18: 24 A/F ≈ 27.7 mm).
- CNC note: "the cutting machine's software normally allows for the width of the cut". Check with the Centre's operator.
- Jamaica claims: builders and clients often still talk in feet and inches; workshop staff share drawing photos in group chats.
- Exam bank T1-21: hole Ø20 +0.2/0 with pin Ø20 0/−0.1 has a minimum clearance of zero. It is still a clearance fit, but some trainees may argue it is line-to-line.

## MEMCOR0103C Maintain quality systems within a team

- **Exam bank errors:** T1-11 has no correct option (the keyed answer "insured for its full value every year" is wrong, and option B is a near copy of it). T1-37's keyed answer ("punishing every mistake ... at the morning meeting") is wrong, and option A is a near copy. Both need rewriting before the bank is used.
- Control-chart run rule given as "often 7 or more points in a row" (some systems use 8). P-13 uses eight points.
- Major and minor non-conformity examples, and P-18 (one lapsed file in twelve = minor), depend on the Centre's audit procedure.
- P-18 assumes a gap of eight months in a welder's continuity record is a lapse. The kit says only "as the code requires". Check the AWS continuity period the Centre uses.
- Calibration intervals "often 6 or 12 months"; record retention "often many years" (no figure).
- Jamaica claim: BSJ publishes and adopts standards; government agencies and large clients may ask for evidence of a quality system.

## MEMCOR0111B Use and care power tools

- RCD trips at 30 mA, GFCI at about 5 mA; Jamaica 110 V and 220 V, 50 Hz; ambulance 110.
- Rim speed formula and the typical 80 m/s rating of bonded discs; run a new disc "often about a minute".
- HSS cutting speeds table (mild steel 25–30, stainless 10–15, aluminium 60–90+ m/min). The bank uses 25 m/min.
- Air tools about 6–6.3 bar (90 psi) at the tool; drain the receiver daily.
- Bench-grinder rest gap "often about 2–3 mm"; lithium-ion batteries stored part-charged; damaged batteries go through "the approved disposal route".
- Pillar drill gloves "often banned by the Centre's rule". The bank's magnetic-drill essay (E04) allows gloves kept away from the cutter, so confirm the Centre's rule.
- Drum-lead ratings in case c1 (13 A unwound, 5 A wound) are case data; real drums vary.

## MEMCOR0121B Classify engineering materials – (basic)

- Jamaica claim: much imported steel is to ASTM grades (A36 plate and sections, A500 hollow sections, A53 pipe), with some to S275 and S355.
- CE formula (IIW) and the guide bands: below 0.40 easy, 0.40–0.45 maybe preheat, above 0.45 preheat and low hydrogen. These are typical, not code values.
- Approximate heat colours, hardening 780–850 °C, tempering colours (pale straw about 220 °C to blue about 300 °C, with typical uses), stress relief 550–650 °C. Charts differ, so match the instructor's chart.
- Physical figures: aluminium conducts heat about 4× steel and copper about 8×; expansion aluminium about 2× and copper about 1.4× steel; austenitic stainless about 1.5×; 316 has 2–3% Mo; weld decay at 450–850 °C.
- The galvanic series is simplified (lead is grouped with brass and copper).
- Cast iron cold method: runs of about 25–50 mm, nickel electrodes, peening.
- Practical 1 results table (file and bend tests on the as-supplied, annealed, normalised, hardened, and hardened-and-tempered samples) describes typical results; real results depend on the bar and the quench.
