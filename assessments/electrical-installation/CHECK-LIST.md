# Electrical Installation Level 2 — points for the instructor to check

Units shared with Welding (communication, maths, OH&S, measuring, marking out, drawings, reports, customer service, quotations, ordering materials, professionalism, entrepreneurship) are listed in `../welding-fabrication/CHECK-LIST.md`.

General: every practical is done on training boards or bays, never live. Run and function tests are done by the instructor on a protected bench. Only earth colours are asked (green, green-yellow or bare); neutral colours are left out because Jamaica mixes North American and IEC practice. Mains is 110/220 V, 50 Hz.

## MEMINS0262D Install distribution panels, metering sockets, terminal mains
- "Terminal mains" is read as the conductors from the meter socket's load terminals to the main breaker and panel. Confirm.
- The meter socket's supply terminals, the meter and the seal are treated as the power company's only. Trainees never touch them, and the practical leaves them empty and labelled. Check this matches the Centre's practice.
- The supply terminals are at the top of the socket.
- A government electrical inspection comes before the power company connects.
- No earth-rod length or earth-resistance pass value is given; only the principle is taught.
- Practical 2 needs an earthing practice area where trainees can drive a rod.

## EEMINS0022B Perform basic testing and inspection on electrical installations
- The usual insulation test voltage is 500 V DC. No minimum pass value is given.
- The GFCI test button is pressed "as the maker's label says".

## EEMMRD0012B Troubleshoot and repair basic electrical/electronic circuits; EEMMRD0022B Dismantle and reassemble electromechanical components
- Groups do dead tests only.
- Practical 2 of EEMMRD0012B powers its board from a 9 V battery, with no mains anywhere. Confirm this fits the Centre's rule.

## MEMINS0062D Terminate and connect specialist cables
- Check these figures:
  - the T568B order;
  - 75 Ω coax for TV;
  - a 100 m Ethernet channel.
- An instrument cable screen is earthed at one end "unless the drawing says otherwise".
- MI (mineral-insulated) cable is included but may be uncommon locally.

## MEMINS0172C Prepare and install basic cable trays
- Short answer 4's 1.2 m support spacing is an arithmetic exercise. The real spacing comes from the maker's tables.

## EEMEDR0011B Interpret and draw standard electrical drawings
- Symbols used: a circle with an X is a lamp, a zigzag or rectangle is a resistor, coils are a transformer. Check these match the symbol set the Centre teaches.
- Question 28 (and essay 6 of MEMINS0011E) explains that a two-way switch is called "three-way" in North American catalogues. Check the term the Centre uses.

## MEMFAB0011D Perform manual soldering/de-soldering of electrical/electronic components
- Question 11 gives 300–350 °C as a common iron temperature for tin-lead solder. Check it against the Centre's solder and stations.

## MEMINS0011E Install, terminate and connect electrical wiring systems
- Question 8: a 220 V water heater goes on its own two-pole breaker.
- Question 27: earth is green or green-and-yellow. Confirm this matches local practice.
- A board's incoming terminals stay live even when the main switch is off.

## MEMINS0051D Cut, bend and install electrical conduits
- The offset multipliers (30° = 2, 45° ≈ 1.4) and the three-bend saddle are geometry.
- Take-up figures are left to the bender maker.
- Saddle spacing, bends between draw-in points and conduit fill are asked as principles ("as the code requires").

## MEMMRD0091D Terminate basic signal and data cables
- Covers twisted pair, coax and screened multi-core cable. Fibre appears in only one short answer; add more if the Centre's unit standard includes fibre terminating.

## Equipment the practicals assume
- Training isolation boards and proving units.
- Low-resistance ohmmeters.
- A hand conduit bender and a die stock.
- Adjust the practicals if any of these are missing.

## MEMINS0092D Install electrical and electronic apparatus, machines and controls
- "Secondary wiring" is read as the control and signal wiring between devices in a panel (question 25, Practical 1). Confirm.

## EEMEDR0012B Use electrical software to draw simple circuits
- Symbols follow the IEC style. Check them against the Centre's software.
- The Ctrl+Z and Esc answers assume Windows.

## EEMINS0012B Interpret electrical standards, specifications and drawings
- The cable table, inverter clearances and the "230/400 V" nameplate are made-up example data, not Jamaican ratings. Change them to local values if they confuse trainees.
- IP65 is given as a safe choice for outdoor fittings.

## EEMELS0011B Apply basic electrical safety
- **Practical 1:** trainees check their tester on a socket outlet of a powered training board, with the instructor present. Decide whether the Centre allows this, or use a proving unit instead.
- Question 22: prove a 110 V outlet dead between all three pairs (L-N, L-E, N-E).
- Electrical fires: CO2 (question 39) and dry powder (short answer 8).
- Practical 2: the shock-rescue role play uses a drill tagged TRAINING that is never plugged in. No real chest compressions are given.

## EEMTEC0011B Apply principles and practices in electrical installation
- 110 V for lights and general outlets (question 9); 220 V for an electric stove (question 10).
- Switch names use both systems: two-way (three-way), strappers or travellers, and "intermediate" switch (questions 18–20). Question 14 assumes AWG wire sizes. Confirm the Centre's terms.
- The instructor connects the board to the protected supply only after the dead tests pass.
